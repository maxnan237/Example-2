# Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) — Business Overview

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02 | Primary source: Form 10-K (fiscal year ended June 30, 2025, filed July 2025)*

*Note: Microsoft's fiscal year ends June 30. "Fiscal 2025" refers to the year ended June 30, 2025. The FY2026 10-K (covering the year ending June 30, 2026) had not yet been filed as of this report's analysis date — Microsoft's Q4 FY2026 earnings are scheduled for July 28, 2026.*

## 1. What the Company Does

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1 / General]: "Microsoft is a technology company committed to making digital technology and artificial intelligence ('AI') available broadly and doing so responsibly. Our mission is to empower every person and every organization on the planet to achieve more." Founded 1975.

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "We operate our business and report our financial performance using three segments: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing." **Note:** segment composition changed in August 2024 — [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm]: "these changes align our segments with how we currently manage our business, most notably bringing the commercial components of Microsoft 365 together in the Productivity and Business Processes segment." FY2024/FY2023 comparatives in the FY2025 filing are recast to this new structure.

- **Employees:** 228,000 full-time (June 30, 2025); 125,000 U.S. / 103,000 international [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]
- **HQ:** Redmond, WA
- **Fiscal year end:** June 30

## 3. Revenue Model

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 1]: "Licenses for on-premises software provide the customer with a right to use the software as it exists when made available to the customer... Revenue from distinct on-premises licenses is recognized upfront at the point in time when the software is made available to the customer." → Perpetual/on-prem licenses: upfront revenue.

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 1]: "Cloud services... are provided on either a subscription or consumption basis. Revenue related to cloud services provided on a subscription basis is recognized ratably over the contract period. Revenue related to cloud services provided on a consumption basis, such as the amount of storage used in a period, is recognized based on the customer utilization of such resources."

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Azure revenue is mainly affected by infrastructure-as-a-service and platform-as-a-service consumption-based services." → Azure is predominantly usage-based, meaning its revenue is more directly tied to customer workload activity (and thus more volatile in principle) than seat-based products like Microsoft 365.

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 1]: "Certain cloud services, primarily Office 365... are accounted for together as one performance obligation. Revenue from Office 365 is recognized ratably over the period in which the cloud services are provided." → M365/Office 365 is ratable subscription revenue.

## 4. Customer Base & Concentration

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Our customers include individual consumers, small and medium organizations, large global enterprises, public-sector institutions, service providers, application developers, and OEMs."

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 18]: "No sales to an individual customer or country other than the United States accounted for more than 10% of revenue for fiscal years 2025, 2024, or 2023." → No material single-customer or single-non-US-country concentration disclosed at the consolidated level.

**Important counterpoint not visible in the 10-K:** (Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) OpenAI is reported to represent approximately 45% of Azure's Remaining Performance Obligations (a $627B backlog figure cited externally), making it, by these reports, effectively Microsoft's largest single cloud customer even though it does not individually cross the 10% *consolidated revenue* threshold disclosed in the filing. This is a meaningful concentration exposure at the Azure/Intelligent Cloud segment level that the consolidated-revenue disclosure does not surface. See [[Bear Case]] for further discussion.

Not found in available filings: exact % of revenue from government customers (flagged as a risk factor but not quantified).

Footnote: "Includes billings to OEMs and certain multinational organizations because of the nature of these businesses and the impracticability of determining the geographic source of the revenue." → The disclosed U.S. figure is inflated by OEM/multinational billing conventions — actual end-customer geographic distribution is likely less U.S.-concentrated than the reported 51.3% suggests.

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 18]: Long-lived assets (predominantly datacenter property/equipment): U.S. $230,069M (FY2025) vs. $186,106M (FY2024) vs. $114,380M (FY2023) — roughly doubled in two years, direct evidence of aggressive domestic AI/cloud infrastructure buildout. International long-lived assets: $141,833M / $115,263M / $72,859M over the same period, also growing rapidly but from a smaller base.

Not found in available filings: revenue broken out by specific country/region beyond the US/non-US split.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

**Important finding — FY2025 10-K removed all named competitors.** [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1, Competition — Intelligent Cloud]: "Azure faces diverse competition from cloud service providers and open source offerings... Our AI offerings compete with AI products from hyperscalers, as well as products from other emerging competitors and other open source offerings, many of which are also current or potential partners." → No named competitors anywhere in the FY2025 filing's competition sections, using deliberately generic language.

**By contrast, the FY2024 10-K explicitly named competitors** (Note: using FY2024 data — FY2025 filing no longer discloses specific competitor names): [MSFT_10K_FY2024.htm, Item 1]:
- Office: "Apple, Cisco Systems, Google, Meta, Proofpoint, Slack, Symantec, Zoom"
- Azure: "Amazon, Broadcom, Google, IBM, Oracle, and open source offerings"
- AI: "hyperscalers such as Amazon and Google... Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, and open source offerings" (notable: OpenAI is named as both a partner and a listed AI competitor)
- Dynamics: "Oracle, Salesforce, SAP, Service Now, UI Path, and WorkDay"
- Windows: "Apple and Google"; Defender for Endpoint vs. "CrowdStrike"
- Gaming: "Amazon, Apple, Meta, and Tencent"
- Search: "Google, OpenAI, and a wide array of websites, social platforms like Meta"
- Analytics: "Databricks, IBM, Oracle, SAP, Snowflake"

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "A well-established ecosystem creates beneficial network effects among users, application developers, and the platform provider that can accelerate growth. Establishing significant scale in the marketplace is necessary to meet consumer demand and to achieve and maintain attractive margins." → Management explicitly frames platform network effects as a double-edged competitive dynamic — it benefits Microsoft's own ecosystems but equally benefits rival platforms.

## 7. Key Products/Technology

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Azure is a comprehensive set of cloud services... Azure AI offerings provide a competitive advantage as companies seek ways to optimize and scale their business with AI. We offer supercomputing power for AI at scale... complemented by our rapidly expanding portfolio of AI cloud services (including the latest models) and hardware, which includes custom-built silicon and strong partnerships with chip manufacturers. Azure AI Foundry is a unified platform for developers to design, customize, and manage AI applications and agents."

**OpenAI partnership — a structurally important relationship:** [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Microsoft and OpenAI maintain a long-term strategic partnership originally established in 2019. Microsoft is a major investor in OpenAI, and the companies have reciprocal revenue-sharing arrangements. We hold rights to OpenAI's intellectual property, including models and infrastructure, for integration into our products. The OpenAI API is exclusive to Azure, runs on Azure, and is available through the Azure OpenAI Service. We also have a right of first refusal on OpenAI's new capacity needs." [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 12]: "We have an investment in OpenAI Global, LLC... and have made total funding commitments of $13 billion. The investment is accounted for under the equity method of accounting."

**Important tension with external reporting:** (Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) 2026 reporting describes a restructuring of the OpenAI agreement that reportedly shifted licensing to non-exclusive terms and altered payment terms — this would represent a material change to the "exclusive to Azure" arrangement described in the FY2025 10-K (filed July 2025). This tension is unresolved in the filings reviewed and is a key item to verify in the next 10-K or 10-Q. See [[Bear Case]] and [[Management Review]].

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Microsoft 365 Commercial is an AI-powered business and productivity solutions platform that brings together Office, Windows, Microsoft 365 Copilot, and Enterprise Mobility + Security."

[MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: LinkedIn ($17,812M FY2025 revenue) — "Talent Solutions, Marketing Solutions, Premium Subscriptions, and Sales Solutions." Gaming ($23,455M) — "Xbox Game Pass, a subscription service and gaming community." Windows and Devices ($17,314M).

**Capital intensity of the AI buildout:** [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Cash Flow Statement]: "Additions to property and equipment (64,551) (44,477) (28,107)" (FY2025/FY2024/FY2023, $M) — capex more than doubled in two years.

## 10. Key Dependencies

1. **AI infrastructure capex intensity.** Capex more than doubled from $28.1B (FY2023) to $64.6B (FY2025) [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm], and (Note: from external source) FY2026 calendar-year capex guidance is ~$190 billion — a scale of ongoing investment that assumes continued, durable AI/cloud demand growth to earn an adequate return.

2. **OpenAI relationship.** [MSFT_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: Azure exclusivity for the OpenAI API, $13B equity-method investment, and IP rights to OpenAI's models — but (Note: from external source) 2026 reporting of a restructured, non-exclusive agreement creates real uncertainty about how durable this arrangement remains, and how concentrated Azure's growth has become in a single AI-lab relationship.

3. **Enterprise agreement renewal cycles.** The 3-year Enterprise Agreement structure creates recurring, contractually-bound renewal/renegotiation events across the customer base — a source of both stability (locked-in multi-year commitments) and risk (concentrated renegotiation windows).

4. **Regulatory exposure across multiple jurisdictions simultaneously** (EU AI Act, EU DMA/DSA, U.S. antitrust, AI export controls) — Microsoft's scale means it is subject to essentially every major digital-market regulatory regime at once.

5. **Talent retention in a highly competitive AI labor market**, disclosed as a risk factor but not separately quantified in the filings reviewed.
