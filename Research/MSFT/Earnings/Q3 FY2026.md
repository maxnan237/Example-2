## Guidance vs. Reality

Revenue and EPS both beat consensus, and Azure growth (+40%) exceeded management's own 37-38% guidance range — a genuine beat-and-raise quarter on every headline growth metric. Despite this, **shares fell more than 3% after-hours on the print itself**, and the stock continued declining sharply through June 2026 — a clear signal that the market's focus has shifted from growth metrics to the capex/free-cash-flow relationship specifically.

**FY2026 (calendar year) capex guidance was set at approximately $190 billion**, including approximately $25 billion attributed to higher component/memory pricing (external source) — a figure well above the $64.6 billion Microsoft spent in the entirety of fiscal year 2025 ([[Financial Deep Dive]]), implying a further significant step-up in the pace of infrastructure investment.

**Q4 FY2026 guidance** (given at this print): revenue guided to $86.7-87.8 billion; Azure growth guided to 39-40% constant currency, with management indicating expected acceleration into the second half of calendar 2026 as capacity comes online.

---

## KPI Trends

The free-cash-flow-to-net-income gap widened materially this quarter: net income of $31.8 billion against free cash flow of only $15.8 billion (down from $20.3 billion a year earlier) — a ratio of roughly 50%, versus a much healthier ~70% FCF-to-OpCF-implied conversion at the full-year FY2025 level ([[Financial Deep Dive]]: FY2025 FCF of $71.6bn against net income of $101.8bn, ~70%). This single-quarter data point is consistent with, and appears to be accelerating, the annual trend already visible in the FY2025 10-K.

AI business surpassing a $37 billion annual revenue run-rate (+123% YoY) is a genuinely strong data point supporting the AI monetization thesis — the open question (per [[Bear Case]]) is whether this run-rate growth is translating into segment-level margin improvement, and the reported Intelligent Cloud margin softness in FY2025 ([[Financial Deep Dive]]) suggests it has not yet done so at the consolidated segment level.

---

## What Changed

1. **The market's reaction function shifted.** A beat-and-raise quarter with accelerating Azure growth nonetheless triggered a negative stock reaction and continued sharp declines through the following two months — confirming that capex/FCF, not growth, is now the primary variable investors are pricing.
2. **Capex guidance stepped up again.** The ~$190 billion calendar-2026 figure represents a further acceleration beyond the already-doubled FY2025 capex level, extending the trend documented in [[Financial Deep Dive]] rather than showing signs of moderation.
3. **AI revenue run-rate disclosure is a new, favorable data point** not available in the FY2025 10-K (which does not break out AI-specific revenue) — this is a useful KPI to track going forward, though its relationship to segment profitability is not yet clear from the sources reviewed.
4. **No update found** on the reported OpenAI exclusivity restructuring specifically in the context of this earnings call — the sources reviewed for this note did not surface direct management commentary confirming or denying the externally-reported change to OpenAI/Azure contract terms.
