## Guidance vs. Reality

Revenue beat consensus meaningfully (+6.8%), continuing the strong topline growth trend seen in the FY2025 annual results ([[Financial Deep Dive]]: +39.1% for FY2025). However, EPS missed by ~12.2% — the fourth consecutive quarter of profit miss, per external reporting — driven by the same dynamic flagged in [[Financial Deep Dive]] and [[Bear Case]]: operating margin compression from credit-loss provisioning and competitive marketing spend. This is a direct, quarter-level continuation of the FY2025 annual trend (operating income growth of 21.7% lagging revenue growth of 39.1%), not a new or surprising development given the filed FY2025 data — though the magnitude and persistence (4 straight misses) is a meaningful escalation worth tracking closely.

**Item-level GMV growth in Brazil (+38% YoY, items sold +56%) is notably strong** — "the fastest in 5 years" per external reporting — which stands in some tension with [[Financial Deep Dive]]'s finding that Brazil's FY2025 segment direct contribution margin declined YoY. This suggests MELI is buying growth in Brazil (via marketing/coupon spend and lower shipping thresholds) rather than growing profitably — consistent with the [[Bear Case]] thesis that competitive intensity (Amazon, Shopee, Temu) is forcing a margin-for-growth trade-off.

---

## KPI Trends

**Credit portfolio growth continues to outpace nearly every other metric.** AUM +77% YoY and total credit portfolio +87% YoY to $14.6 billion — an acceleration from the already-rapid FY2025 annual pace (net loans receivable roughly doubling year-over-year, [[Financial Deep Dive]]). Credit card TPV growth of +90% YoY, with 2.7 million new cards issued in a single quarter, indicates the credit business is scaling even faster than the FY2025 annual figures suggested.

**Asset quality signal is mixed but not alarming.** The 15-90 day NPL ratio of ~8.0% is reported as "broadly stable YoY," and credit card NPL reportedly improved 80bps YoY — this is a modestly reassuring data point against the [[Bear Case]] concern about credit-quality deterioration, though it does not resolve the underlying NIMAL-compression trend, which is a profitability (not delinquency) metric and was not specifically updated in the external sources reviewed for this note.

**Market reaction was sharply negative despite the revenue beat.** (Note: from external source) The stock reportedly fell ~11-13% on the print — a clear signal that margin trajectory, not growth, is now the dominant variable investors are pricing, consistent with the pattern seen at MSFT and CSU elsewhere in this vault.

## What Changed

1. **Margin miss became a persistent, multi-quarter pattern** (4 straight quarters, per external reporting) rather than a single-quarter anomaly — raising the bar for what would need to happen to restore market confidence in near-term margin recovery.
2. **Brazil GMV growth accelerated to a 5-year high** even as the FY2025 annual data already showed segment margin compression — suggesting the growth-for-margin trade-off intensified further in Q1 2026, not just persisted at the FY2025 pace.
3. **Credit portfolio growth accelerated** (AUM +77% YoY vs. the roughly-doubling annual pace implied by FY2025 data) — the fintech/credit engine continues scaling rapidly, keeping the central NIMAL/asset-quality question from [[Bear Case]] fully in focus.
4. This is the first full quarter reported under new CEO Ariel Szarfsztejn's leadership (effective January 1, 2026) — no major strategic pivot was reported in the sources reviewed, suggesting continuity with the prior growth-and-reinvestment approach so far.
