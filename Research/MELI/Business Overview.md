# MercadoLibre, Inc. (MELI) — Business Overview

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02 | Primary source: Form 10-K (fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, filed February 2026)*

---

## 1. What the Company Does

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "MercadoLibre, Inc.... is the leading online commerce and fintech ecosystem in Latin America. Our e-commerce platform is the leader in the region based on gross merchandise volume ('GMV'), and our fintech platform is the leader in monthly active users ('MAUs') among fintech companies in Argentina, Chile and Mexico, and the second largest in Brazil."

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Mercado Libre's e-commerce platform is present in 18 countries... and our fintech platform, Mercado Pago, is present in 8 countries (Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Uruguay and Ecuador)." → The two platforms have different geographic footprints; fintech is narrower and more selectively rolled out.

- **Employees:** 123,670 (Dec 31, 2025), up from 84,207 (2024) and 58,313 (2023) — nearly quadrupled in four years [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]
- **HQ:** Delaware-incorporated, administrative headquarters in Montevideo, Uruguay and Buenos Aires, Argentina
- **Fiscal year end:** December 31
- **Reporting currency:** U.S. dollars, U.S. GAAP

## 3. Revenue Model

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Our Commerce business is comprised of two primary revenue streams: Services and Product Sales... Our Fintech business is comprised of three primary revenue streams: (a) Financial services and income... (b) Credit Revenues... and (c) Fintech Product Sales."

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: Commerce monetization: "marketplace fees that include final value fees and flat fees... shipping fees... storage fees, ad sales fees (CPC/CPM basis), classifieds fees, MELI+ subscription fees."

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: Fintech monetization: "commissions representing a percentage of the payment volume processed... charged to sellers in connection with off-Marketplace platform transactions; commissions from additional fees... when a buyer elects to pay in installments... interest, cash advances and fees from credit cards, merchant, consumer and asset-backed loans."

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: "Our Fintech revenues grew 46.2%, from $8,618 million... to $12,599 million... mainly generated by: an increase of $2,258 million in our Credits revenues, mainly as a consequence of higher originations [and] an increase of $1,716 million in our revenues from Financial services and income... as a result of a 41.3% increase in our total payment volume."

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: "Although we also process payments on the Marketplace, we do not charge sellers an added commission for this service, as it is already included in the Marketplace final value fee." → On-platform payments are bundled into the commerce take-rate; only off-platform payment processing is separately monetized.

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: "For the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023, no single customer accounted for more than 5.0% of our net revenues and financial income."

**NIMAL is declining sharply** — from 36.2% (FY2023) to 22.4% (FY2025) — even as the credit portfolio has grown rapidly. This is the single most important KPI trend for assessing whether MELI's fast-growing credit business is scaling profitably or scaling at the expense of unit economics (see [[Financial Deep Dive]] and [[Bear Case]]).

**Credit portfolio (balance sheet):** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 8]: total net loans receivable grew from $2,694M (Dec 2023) to $4,895M (Dec 2024) to $9,365M (Dec 2025) — the portfolio has roughly doubled year-over-year for two consecutive years.

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: "Provision for doubtful accounts $3,091 [2025] $1,858 [2024] $1,050 [2023]... As a percentage of net revenues and financial income 10.7% [2025] 8.9% [2024] 7.0% [2023]." → Provisioning intensity is rising as the credit book scales.

Not found in available filings: an explicit count of "unique active sellers" (only "unique active buyers" is disclosed); a discrete registered Mercado Pago digital-account holder count.

## 6. Competitive Landscape

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "In 2025, several new global and regional entrants, including rapidly expanding Asian e-commerce platforms, gained significant market share in Latin America through low-price strategies, direct-from-manufacturer supply chains and cross-border logistics models." → The filing uses generic "Asian e-commerce platforms" language rather than naming Shein, Shopee, or Temu specifically.

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Mercado Libre's Marketplace currently competes with a number of companies operating throughout Latin America, including: traditional brick and mortar retailers, e-commerce and omnichannel retailers... social media platforms... companies that provide e-commerce related services." — categorical, not company-specific.

**One named competitor found, in a regulatory (not competitive) context:** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: describing a closed Mexican antitrust proceeding, "The authority identified potential concerns related to the operation of Buy Box algorithms and the integration of logistics services by Amazon Mexico and Mercado Libre, however, no corrective measures were imposed and the proceeding was closed." Separately, AWS and Google Cloud are named as key cloud infrastructure vendors, not competitors.

**Named competitors NOT found in available filings**: Shopee, Shein, Temu, Magazine Luiza/Magalu, Americanas, and Nubank do not appear anywhere in any of the three 10-Ks reviewed (confirmed via full-text search). (Note: from external source) these are, however, frequently cited externally as MELI's most significant competitive threats — a meaningful gap between the company's own disclosure and the competitive dynamics discussed in outside reporting. See [[Industry Scan]] and [[Bear Case]].

[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "Barriers-to-entry for large, well-established technology companies are relatively low, and current and new competitors can launch new sites at a relatively low cost using commercially available software." → A notable, explicit, company-acknowledged admission that undercuts a pure network-effect moat narrative.

## 7. Key Products/Technology

- **Marketplace**: predominantly third-party (3P) sellers (>90% of GMV), with first-party (1P) sales in select categories (<10% of GMV) [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1].
- **Mercado Envios** (logistics): [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "built around fulfillment centers (which accounts for more than half of shipments)... a network of thousands of partner stores ('MELI Places')... dedicated aircraft, trucks and thousands of last-mile delivery vans, the vast majority of which are owned and operated by our third-party carriers."
- **Mercado Ads**: sponsored listings, brand carousels, display/video ads (including off-platform inventory).
- **Mercado Pago**: digital account, POS/QR for offline merchants, cards, insurance, savings/investment, crypto (Brazil/Mexico/Chile), and the new **Meli Dólar stablecoin** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "In 2024 and 2025 we launched 'Meli Dólar,' a stablecoin that is pegged to the US dollar, in Brazil, Mexico and Chile," with the business migrated from Uruguay to Bermuda in September 2025 following the BMA's issuance of "Class M Digital Asset Business licenses" (August 18, 2025).
- **Mercado Crédito** (lending): merchant, consumer, credit-card, and asset-backed loans in Argentina, Brazil, Mexico, and Chile; credit card rollout: Brazil (2021), Mexico (2023), Argentina (2025).
- **Mercado Shops discontinued** as of December 31, 2025, migrated to "Mi Página," embedded directly in the Marketplace.
- **AI adoption**: [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: "Approximately 95% of our employees have adopted GenAI tools in their work... about 30% of the code that reaches production is written with the use of AI."

## 8. Regulatory Environment

MELI's fintech operations are separately licensed across nearly every country it operates in — [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1, Government Regulation]:
- **Brazil:** BACEN-authorized electronic money issuer (since 2018) and financial institution (Mercado Crédito, since 2020); new capital rules effective January 2025 (10.50% regulatory capital ratio); credit card interest-rate cap in force since January 2, 2024 ("interest and financial charges may not exceed 100% of the original debt amount").
- **Mexico:** CNBV-regulated under the 2018 Fintech Law; IFPE (Electronic Payment Institution) license since May 2022; application filed September 11, 2024 to become a full multiple banking institution — "still pending approval" as of the FY2025 filing date.
- **Argentina:** Registered as a payment service provider (2020) and non-financial loan provider; filed for an Argentine banking license June 17, 2025 — "under review" as of the filing date.
- **Chile, Colombia, Uruguay:** each has separate country-specific fintech/prepaid-card/financing licenses.

**Currency/capital controls:** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "In certain countries where we operate, we need governmental authorization to pay invoices to a foreign supplier or send money abroad due to foreign exchange restrictions"; Argentina specifically has had "extensive exchange controls."

**Data privacy:** country-specific laws named across Brazil (LGPD), Argentina, Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Uruguay, Peru, China, and the U.S.

**Material litigation:** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 8, Note 14]: "As of December 31, 2025, the Company had accounted for estimated liabilities... of $129 million (net of judicial deposits)... subject to certain legal actions considered... reasonably possible of resulting in a loss for an aggregate amount up to $449 million." The largest disclosed matter is a Brazil-Argentina double-taxation withholding tax dispute dating to 2014, for which "Management's opinion, based on the opinion of external legal counsel, is that the risk of losing the case is probable."

## 10. Key Dependencies

1. **Extreme country concentration.** Brazil, Argentina, and Mexico together account for 95.6% of revenue — any single-country macro or regulatory shock has outsized consolidated impact.

2. **Argentina currency/inflation exposure.** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "Argentina's annual inflation rate for the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023 was 31.5%, 117.8% and 211.4%, respectively, and Argentina's official exchange rate against the U.S. dollar increased 41.0%, 27.7% and 356.3%, respectively." Argentina has been treated as a highly inflationary economy under U.S. GAAP since July 1, 2018.

3. **Credit/lending risk as the book scales rapidly.** Net loans receivable nearly doubled year-over-year for two consecutive years, while NIMAL fell from 36.2% to 22.4% over the same period — a structural dependency on continued credit-quality discipline as the portfolio matures.

4. **Third-party logistics/carrier reliance.** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]: the "vast majority" of last-mile delivery vehicles are owned and operated by third-party carriers, not MELI itself.

5. **Regulatory approval pipeline.** Pending banking-license applications in both Mexico and Argentina represent a material, binary regulatory dependency for the fintech business's next stage of growth.

6. **Cloud infrastructure vendor concentration.** [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: named reliance on Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud Platform as "the largest" cloud providers used.
