# MercadoLibre, Inc. (MELI) — Industry Scan

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02*

---

## 1. Market Size and Growth

(Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) The Latin American e-commerce market is valued at $1.61 trillion (2025), estimated to reach $1.78 trillion (2026); a narrower "e-commerce sales" definition puts the market at $215.31 billion in 2026, growing roughly 1.5x faster than the global average, with a longer-term CAGR of ~9.4-10.9% through 2029-2034. Argentina, Brazil, and Mexico together represented ~85% of regional e-commerce sales in 2025 — closely mirroring MELI's own 95.6% revenue concentration in those same three countries ([[Business Overview]]).

(Note: from external source) The LatAm digital payments/fintech market is valued at $48.7 billion (2026), projected to reach $240.2 billion by 2035 (19.4% CAGR) — a substantially faster growth rate than the core e-commerce market, consistent with MELI's own fintech revenue outgrowing commerce revenue in FY2025 (+46.2% vs. +34.0%, [[Business Overview]]).

MELI's own filings do not provide independent third-party market-size estimates — not found in available filings.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

As detailed in [[Business Overview]], MELI's own 10-K uses only generic, categorical competitor language — it does not name Shopee, Shein, Temu, Nubank, or Magazine Luiza anywhere in the three filings reviewed. External reporting fills this gap with specific, material detail:

(Note: from external source) **Amazon** has invested more than $10 billion in Brazil (~25% of that total in the last 18 months alone), waived fees for FBA-style sellers, and partnered with Nu Holdings on credit/payments — a direct, well-capitalized challenge in MELI's largest market. **Shopee** now represents an estimated ~15% of Brazilian e-commerce GMV, up from effectively 0% in 2020. **Temu** holds an estimated ~5% share, and **TikTok Shop** is reportedly entering the LatAm market. MELI's disclosed response has been "heavy coupon/marketing spend" (one source cites ~$19 million in coupons in a single reported period) and lower free-shipping thresholds — directly pressuring margins (see [[Financial Deep Dive]] and [[Bear Case]]).

## 3. Regulatory Environment

See [[Business Overview]] for full detail on country-by-country fintech licensing. Two recent regulatory developments are worth flagging beyond what's in the filings:

(Note: from external source) **Mexico** eliminated its ~$50 USD de minimis customs exemption effective January 1, 2026 (except for US/Canada shipments under USMCA), replacing it with a flat 19% "tasa global" tax on courier shipments up to $2,500, and — as part of Mexico's broader 2026 tax reform — now requires digital platforms like MELI to withhold 50% VAT plus 2.5% income tax on B2B seller transactions where sellers provide a Mexican tax ID. This is a material, recent compliance and margin consideration not reflected in the FY2025 10-K's forward-looking risk language, though it is broadly consistent with the filing's disclosed "government regulation" risk factor.

(Note: from external source) **Argentina** under the Milei government: the economy grew 4.4% in 2025 (a rebound from -1.7% in 2024); a new FX band regime took effect January 2, 2026 (band widening ~2.5-2.8%/month tied to lagged inflation); inflation has fallen to ~31.8% YoY (November 2025) but was reportedly ticking back up; reserves remain thin, with some risk of renewed currency pressure. This is directly relevant to the currency risk described in [[Business Overview]] and [[Bear Case]].

## 4. Industry Structural Notes

- MELI's own filings notably do not engage with the specific competitive threats (Amazon's Brazil investment, Shopee's share gains, Temu, TikTok Shop) that external reporting identifies as the primary drivers of its recent margin compression — this is a meaningful disclosure gap for investors relying solely on the 10-K.
- The LatAm fintech market's much faster projected growth rate (19.4% CAGR) versus core e-commerce (~9.4-10.9% CAGR) supports MELI's strategic emphasis on fintech/credit as the primary growth engine, but this also concentrates more of the company's future growth in a segment (credit) where asset-quality and provisioning risk is rising, not falling (see [[Financial Deep Dive]]).
- Argentina's macro trajectory under the Milei government is a genuine wildcard for MELI given the country represents ~20.6% of consolidated revenue — a positive economic rebound (4.4% GDP growth in 2025) is a tailwind, but the country's history of currency volatility means this could reverse quickly, as reflected in MELI's own risk factor disclosures.
