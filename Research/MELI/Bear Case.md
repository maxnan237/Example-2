# MercadoLibre, Inc. (MELI) — Bear Case

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02*

---

## 1. Thesis Killer

MELI's premium valuation (46.6x trailing P/E, second-highest among direct e-commerce/fintech peers, [[Valuation Check]]) has always depended on the market believing MELI's marketplace-plus-fintech ecosystem carries a durable competitive moat that justifies growth-stock multiples indefinitely. The company's own risk factors directly undercut that belief: [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "Barriers-to-entry for large, well-established technology companies are relatively low, and current and new competitors can launch new sites at a relatively low cost using commercially available software." If Amazon's reported >$10 billion Brazil investment (external source), combined with Shopee's rapid share gains (from ~0% to ~15% of Brazilian e-commerce GMV since 2020, external source), continues eroding MELI's largest market — and Brazil's segment direct contribution margin already declined YoY in FY2025 despite 33.3% revenue growth ([[Financial Deep Dive]]) — the single most likely way a long-term holder loses money is a continued, structural margin de-rating in MELI's core market, not a one-off competitive skirmish.

---

## 2. Structural Weaknesses

### 2a. Extreme three-country concentration with genuine macro/currency fragility
[MELI_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "Brazil, Argentina and Mexico, which together accounted for 95.6% and 95.7% of our net revenues and financial income for 2025 and 2024." Argentina alone experienced 211.4% inflation and 356.3% currency depreciation as recently as FY2023 [MELI_10K_FY2025.htm] — while conditions have improved under the current government (Note: from external source, 4.4% GDP growth in 2025), the country's history argues for treating any stability as fragile, not structural, especially given (Note: from external source) reserves remain thin and inflation was reportedly ticking back up as of late 2025.

### 2b. Rapidly scaling credit business with compressing per-dollar profitability
[[Financial Deep Dive]]: NIMAL fell from 36.2% (FY2023) to 22.4% (FY2025) even as net loans receivable nearly doubled year-over-year for two consecutive years ($2,694M→$4,895M→$9,365M) and provision for doubtful accounts grew 194% over the same period ($1,050M→$3,091M). A credit book growing this fast, with declining marginal profitability, is a structural early-warning pattern that deserves close scrutiny — it is not yet a credit-quality crisis (allowance ratios are actually improving slightly, from 28.7% to 25.1% of gross portfolio), but the direction of NIMAL is the wrong one for a business the market is valuing at a premium multiple.

### 2c. Regulatory dependency for the next stage of fintech growth
[[Business Overview]]: pending banking-license applications in both Mexico (filed September 2024, "still pending approval") and Argentina (filed June 2025, "under review") represent binary, government-controlled gates on MELI's ability to expand its most profitable, fastest-growing business line — a dependency largely outside management's direct control.

## 3. Assumptions the Bull Case Takes for Granted

1. **That Brazil's segment margin compression in FY2025 is a temporary competitive response, not a structural repricing.** The bull case requires this margin to recover; the bear case only requires it to persist at current levels or worsen.

2. **That the declining NIMAL trend (36.2%→22.4%) reflects normal portfolio-maturation dynamics rather than deteriorating underlying credit quality that has not yet fully shown up in reported metrics.** (Note: from external source) management itself has flagged that the true asset quality of newer, longer-duration, lower-rate "riskier cohort" loan vintages "won't show up in NPL data for several quarters" — an explicit, company-acknowledged lag risk that the bull case must assume resolves favorably.

3. **That new CEO Ariel Szarfsztejn maintains the same strategic discipline associated with founder Marcos Galperin's two-decade tenure**, despite this being genuinely unproven — Galperin's continued role as Executive Chairman provides some continuity assurance, but this is a real, unresolved succession question, similar in kind to the CSU leadership-transition risk elsewhere in this vault.

4. **That Argentina's currency/macro stability under the current FX-band regime holds**, rather than reverting to the extreme volatility seen as recently as FY2023 (211.4% inflation) — Argentina represents 20.6% of consolidated revenue, so this is not a peripheral risk.

5. **That the ~25.7% implied upside to average analyst price targets (external source) reflects genuine undervaluation rather than stale targets** — a pattern that has appeared with mixed reliability elsewhere in this vault (e.g., MSFT, CSU), where some cited targets were later revised down as new information emerged.

## 4. Permanent Impairment Scenarios

### Scenario A: Brazil margin compression proves structural
If Amazon, Shopee, and Temu continue gaining share in Brazil (52.6% of consolidated revenue) and MELI is forced into a sustained, elevated marketing/coupon spend to defend share, Brazil's segment economics could permanently reset to a lower-margin equilibrium — directly reducing the earnings power the market is pricing at a premium multiple.

### Scenario B: Credit portfolio asset-quality deterioration
If the "riskier cohort" of recent loan vintages (per external management commentary) proves worse than underwritten once it seasons, provision for doubtful accounts could spike well beyond the already-elevated 10.7% of revenue seen in FY2025, materially impairing both reported earnings and MELI's ability to continue funding rapid credit-book growth via debt markets.

### Scenario C: Argentina macro reversal
A return to Argentina's FY2023-level currency volatility (356.3% depreciation, 211.4% inflation) would directly reduce the USD-translated value of ~20.6% of MELI's consolidated revenue and could reintroduce large foreign-currency losses to the income statement, as seen in FY2023's $(615)M foreign currency loss line.

## Summary

MELI remains the clear regional leader in both e-commerce and fintech across Latin America, with strong absolute growth in users, GMV, and cash flow. But the bear case here is not speculative — it is grounded directly in the FY2025 financial statements: Brazil's segment margin compressed even as revenue grew strongly, the credit business's per-dollar profitability has fallen by roughly a third over two years while the book has nearly doubled annually, and the company's own risk factors explicitly acknowledge low barriers to entry against exactly the kind of well-capitalized competitors (Amazon, Shopee, Temu) now gaining share in its largest market. Layer on extreme three-country concentration, Argentina's history of currency volatility, and an unproven new CEO, and the combination of a premium valuation with genuinely deteriorating segment-level economics is a real, not merely theoretical, risk to the investment thesis.
