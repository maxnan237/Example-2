## 2. Capital Allocation

TME's capital allocation has three visible pillars, all confirmed in filings: buybacks, dividends, and M&A.

**Buybacks.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5.B]: Four successive programs — 2019 (US$400m, only ~US$19m used), 2021 (US$1.0bn, fully completed, ~133.8m ADSs repurchased), 2023 (US$500m, fully utilized), and 2025 (US$1.0bn, 2-year window from March 21, 2025, currently active). Annual buyback spend: US$175m (2023) → US$261m (2024) → US$64m (2025) — a notable slowdown in 2025 despite launching a new US$1bn authorization, worth monitoring for whether management views the stock as less attractively priced or is preserving cash for the Ximalaya deal.

**Dividends.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5 / Note 34]: First-ever dividend policy adopted May 11, 2024. Per-ADS dividend has risen in each of the three declarations since: US$0.137 (FY2023) → US$0.18 (FY2024) → US$0.24 (FY2025, declared March 2026) — a ~75% increase over three years, signaling management confidence in durable free cash flow generation (FCF of RMB9.0-9.2bn in both FY2024 and FY2025 per [[Financial Deep Dive]]).

**M&A — Ximalaya acquisition.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.A]: Announced June 10, 2025, cash consideration of US$1.26 billion plus Class A shares of up to ~5.57% of TME's total outstanding shares. (Note: from external source) Closed May 18, 2026, subject to SAMR conditions prohibiting new exclusive audio licensing agreements. This is the largest single capital commitment in TME's disclosed history and materially changes the company's audio-content footprint (adds China's leading standalone long-form audio platform) — but was funded partly via new RMB3.0bn bank borrowing drawn in Q1 2026 ([TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Note 35]), TME's first material use of new leverage in years despite sitting on RMB38bn of cash and term deposits.

**Assessment:** Capital allocation is shareholder-friendly on the returns side (rising dividend, sustained buybacks) and reasonably disciplined (net cash balance sheet even after taking on new Ximalaya-related debt), but the 2025 buyback slowdown alongside a large, competitively-defensive acquisition suggests management is prioritizing consolidation of market position over pure capital return at the current juncture — a reasonable but not risk-free choice given the MAU-decline backdrop discussed in [[Moat Analysis]].

## 3. Insider Ownership and Related-Party Structure

**Tencent Holdings — controlling shareholder.** (Note: from external source) Tencent Holdings (0700.HK) holds approximately 52.4% equity interest and greater than 90% of voting power in TME via a dual-class share structure — TME is not independently controlled by its public (ADS-holding) shareholders. [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D. Risk Factors]: "Tencent, our controlling shareholder, has had and will continue to have effective control over the outcome of shareholder actions in our company. The interests of Tencent may not be aligned with the interests of our other shareholders and holders of the ADSs." This is a direct, filed acknowledgment from the company itself that governance risk to minority holders is real, not hypothetical.

**Spotify — passive cross-shareholder.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 7. Major Shareholders]: Spotify AB holds 282,830,698 Class A ordinary shares (9.0% of total ordinary shares, 1.1% of aggregate voting power), with voting power over those shares assigned to Tencent under a 2017 Investor Agreement — reinforcing, not diluting, Tencent's effective control.

**TME's stake in Universal Music Group.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.A]: TME (via a Tencent-led consortium) originally held an indirect ~10% stake in UMG (acquired March 2020); following a March 2025 distribution-in-kind, TME now holds a **direct 2% equity interest in UMG** — a modest but real cross-holding in a key content supplier, which somewhat aligns TME's incentives with the labels it licenses from.

**Recent insider transactions.** (Note: from external source) A Form 144 disclosed a proposed sale of 500,000 ADS by an insider (acquired via employee stock option exercise, dated August 27, 2025), aggregate value ~US$12.6 million, executed through Futu Securities. No other specific insider buying/selling was found via external search. **Not found in available filings:** a full Form 3/4/5-level accounting of officer/director transactions — this analysis relies on a single external Form 144 data point and should not be treated as comprehensive.

## 4. Guidance Credibility

TME does not appear to issue formal quarterly or annual financial guidance in the manner typical of U.S. issuers (this is common among Chinese ADR filers) — **not found in available filings.** The closest analog is management's qualitative MD&A commentary in each 20-F on drivers of the following year's expected trends (e.g., subscription growth, SVIP mix shift), which has generally proven directionally accurate based on the three years of filed results reviewed (music subscription revenue growth has been consistently the stated and realized primary driver across FY2023-FY2025).

(Note: from external source) TME's decision to **discontinue quarterly disclosure of MAU, paying users, and ARPPU starting Q1 2026** (moving to annual-only KPI disclosure) is a meaningful reduction in external monitoring transparency, disclosed via TME's Q1 2026 earnings release rather than in the FY2025 20-F reviewed here (the 20-F was filed April 17, 2026, before the Q1 2026 earnings release of May 12, 2026). This should be read as a governance/transparency downgrade for investors tracking the core MAU-decline narrative discussed in [[Moat Analysis]] and [[Bear Case]] — it is harder, going forward, to independently verify quarter-to-quarter whether the MAU decline is stabilizing or accelerating.

---

## 5. Overall Assessment

Management has been a credible steward of capital allocation — rising dividends, disciplined buybacks, a large but strategically logical acquisition (Ximalaya) funded conservatively relative to the balance sheet — and has grown adjusted profitability meaningfully over the three years reviewed. However, two governance-adjacent concerns weigh on the overall picture: (1) Tencent's >90% voting control means minority ADS holders have essentially no influence over major corporate decisions, a structural feature acknowledged by the company itself as a risk factor; and (2) the discontinuation of quarterly user-metric disclosure — occurring in the same period as a well-documented MAU-decline/competitive-share narrative — reduces the market's ability to monitor the single most important operating trend in the business in real time. Neither is disqualifying on its own, but together they argue for a somewhat higher discount on trust in forward disclosure than the historical execution record alone would suggest.
