## 5. Summary

| Scenario | Fair Value | Return |
|---|---|---|
| Bull | ~$15.40 | +78% |
| Base | ~$10.90 | +26% |
| Bear | ~$5.75 | -34% |

**Risk/reward: Asymmetric up**, on a simple average-of-scenarios basis — but this asymmetry should be treated cautiously. The bull and base cases both require the MAU-decline narrative to stabilize, which is precisely the open question the market has been punishing the stock for since March 2026, and which has become *harder to verify* now that TME has discontinued quarterly MAU disclosure ([[Management Review]]). The stock is statistically cheap relative to peers and sell-side targets, but "cheap because the market doesn't trust the growth/competitive story" is a different setup than "cheap and mispriced."

## Key Valuation Considerations

The market is currently pricing TME at a discount to nearly every direct comparable (NetEase aside) and well below sell-side consensus targets, reflecting genuine uncertainty about whether the FY2023-FY2025 MAU decline (589m → 547m) is a stabilizing mix-shift toward higher-value users or the early stage of a Soda Music-driven share-loss spiral that TME's own filings do not name as a risk (see [[Industry Scan]] and [[Bear Case]]). The balance sheet (net cash of ~$4.9B against an $8.7-8.8B enterprise value) provides real downside support and funds a growing dividend, which caps some of the bear-case severity relative to a levered competitor facing the same pressures.

What would change the view: (1) resumption of transparent quarterly MAU/paying-user disclosure showing stabilization, (2) evidence the Ximalaya integration is driving cross-platform user retention rather than just adding standalone audio revenue, (3) resolution of the NetEase antitrust suit without a structural remedy that further restricts TME's licensing flexibility, and (4) clarity on whether the SAMR-imposed ban on new exclusive licensing deals (tied to the Ximalaya approval) applies narrowly to that transaction or constrains TME's broader content strategy.
