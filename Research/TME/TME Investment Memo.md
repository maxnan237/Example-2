# TME Investment Memo

**Tencent Music Entertainment Group (NYSE: TME) | 2026-07-02**

---

## The Business in 30 Seconds

TME is China's largest online music entertainment platform by MAU, operating an integrated ecosystem of streaming (QQ Music, Kugou Music, Kuwo Music), karaoke/social entertainment (WeSing), and long-form audio (Lazy Audio, plus the newly-acquired Ximalaya). It is a Cayman Islands-incorporated, Tencent-controlled company operating in China via a VIE structure, generating 81.2% of revenue from online music subscriptions/advertising and 18.8% from a shrinking social entertainment segment [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B].

---

## What's Going Right

**Monetization is compounding even as the user base shrinks.** Paying ratio rose from 17.1% to 22.9% and monthly ARPPU from RMB10.0 to RMB11.8 over FY2023-FY2025 [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5], with SVIP subscribers surpassing 20 million.

**Margins are expanding sharply.** Gross margin rose from 42.3% to 44.2% YoY in FY2025; operating profit grew 53.4% YoY to RMB13,364m [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5.A] — though a portion of this reflects a one-time RMB2,285m investment gain (see [[Financial Deep Dive]]).

**Balance sheet is fortress-like.** Net cash of ~RMB34.5bn (~$4.9B) against total debt of just RMB3,497m [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Consolidated Balance Sheets], funding a dividend that has grown ~75% across three declarations (US$0.137 → US$0.18 → US$0.24 per ADS) and an active $1bn buyback program.

**Valuation discount to peers.** Trailing P/E of 10.5x and forward 8.77x sit well below Spotify (28.8x fwd), Tencent Holdings (12.1x fwd), and Warner Music Group (49.7x trailing) (Note: from external source). Sell-side average price target of ~$16 implies ~85% upside from the $8.66 price (Note: from external source).

**Ximalaya acquisition adds scale in long-form audio**, closing May 18, 2026 (Note: from external source) for US$1.26bn cash plus shares — consolidating China's leading standalone audio platform rather than competing with it.

---

## What Could Go Wrong

**MAU has declined for two straight years** — 589m (FY2023) → 570m (FY2024) → 547m (FY2025) [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5] — while a well-capitalized new entrant reportedly leveraging short-video distribution (ByteDance's Soda Music, per external reporting) has emerged as a competitive threat that **TME's own filings do not name anywhere.**

**Core content licensing is explicitly non-exclusive.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "Our licensing agreements with music labels and music copyright owners are on a non-exclusive basis" — meaning TME's content catalog is not a defensible moat against NetEase Cloud Music or new entrants.

**Quarterly KPI transparency was just discontinued.** Starting Q1 2026, TME stopped quarterly MAU/paying-user/ARPPU disclosure, moving to annual-only reporting (Note: from external source) — reducing investors' ability to verify in real time whether the MAU decline is stabilizing.

**Governance risk from controlling shareholder.** Tencent holds >90% of voting power (Note: from external source); [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D]: "The interests of Tencent may not be aligned with the interests of our other shareholders." Minority ADS holders have no practical influence over major decisions.

**Active antitrust litigation and regulatory constraints on growth levers.** NetEase Cloud Music sued TME in June 2024 alleging abuse of market dominance in music licensing [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 8]; separately, SAMR's approval of the Ximalaya deal reportedly bars TME from pursuing new exclusive audio licensing agreements (Note: from external source) — closing off a path to widening the moat via M&A.

**VIE and China-ADR structural risk.** ADS holders' economic claim runs through contractual VIE arrangements, not direct equity ownership [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.C], with attendant HFCAA/PCAOB delisting risk disclosed as an ongoing risk factor.

## Decision

**Current stance: Monitor**

TME asks investors to believe that a two-year decline in its core user metric is a monetization-driven mix-shift rather than the early stage of competitive share loss to a rival the company itself does not name in its regulatory filings. The valuation case is genuinely attractive on the numbers: a net-cash balance sheet, single-digit forward earnings multiple, a dividend that has grown 75% in three years, and sell-side targets implying substantial upside. But the market's ability to verify the central operating question — is the MAU decline stabilizing? — has just gotten harder, not easier, with TME's move to annual-only user-metric disclosure starting Q1 2026. At the current price (~$8.66), the thesis becomes more clearly compelling once there is direct evidence (even qualitative, from earnings-call commentary) that management is addressing the Soda Music-style competitive threat and that the Ximalaya integration is driving incremental engagement rather than just adding standalone revenue. Until then, this is a statistically cheap stock with an unresolved core-metric question and reduced transparency to track it — a position size, not a full conviction bet, at most.

**Key triggers to watch:**
- Resumption of more-frequent-than-annual MAU/paying-user disclosure showing stabilization
- Direct management commentary addressing competitive share loss to new entrants
- Resolution of the NetEase antitrust litigation
- First full-year results showing the Ximalaya integration's contribution

**Key risks to monitor:**
- Accelerating MAU decline with no interim visibility to detect it
- Adverse antitrust/regulatory outcome further restricting TME's licensing flexibility
- Tencent-related-party dealings that could disadvantage minority ADS holders
- Broader China-ADR/VIE structural or delisting risk unrelated to TME's own operating performance
