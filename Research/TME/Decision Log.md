# Tencent Music Entertainment Group (TME) — Decision Log

*Initiated: 2026-07-02*

---

## Investment Thesis Summary

TME is China's largest online music platform by MAU, monetizing a shrinking-but-higher-value user base through subscriptions, advertising, and social entertainment/virtual gifts, trading at a discount to global and domestic peers on a net-cash balance sheet with a rapidly growing dividend.

**Bull thesis:** Rising ARPPU (RMB10.0 → RMB11.8/month, FY2023-FY2025) and paying-user conversion (17.1% → 22.9%) demonstrate durable monetization power even as MAU has declined; the Ximalaya acquisition adds scale in long-form audio; a net-cash balance sheet (~$4.9B net cash against an ~$8.7-8.8B enterprise value) funds a dividend that has grown ~75% in three declarations and an active $1bn buyback program; the stock trades at a meaningful discount to sell-side price targets (avg. ~$16 vs. $8.66 price) and to comparable peers (Spotify, Tencent, WMG) on both P/E and EV/EBITDA.

**Bear thesis:** MAU has declined for two consecutive years (589m → 547m) while a new, well-capitalized competitor (ByteDance's Soda Music, per external reporting — not named in TME's own filings) is reportedly gaining share via short-video-driven distribution; TME's core content licensing is explicitly non-exclusive, leaving no structural moat to defend share; Tencent's >90% voting control and the VIE structure both limit minority-shareholder recourse; an active NetEase antitrust suit and SAMR-imposed restrictions on new exclusive licensing (tied to the Ximalaya deal) constrain TME's remaining strategic options; and TME has just discontinued the quarterly MAU/paying-user/ARPPU disclosure that would let investors independently verify whether the decline is stabilizing.

## Decision Framework

**To buy at ~$8.66:**
- Confirmation (via the next 20-F or any resumed interim disclosure) that MAU decline is decelerating, not accelerating
- Evidence Ximalaya integration is additive to the core platform (cross-promotion, retention) rather than merely bolt-on revenue
- No adverse structural remedy from the NetEase antitrust litigation

**To avoid:**
- Continued MAU decline with no visibility (given discontinued quarterly disclosure) into whether it is stabilizing
- Evidence Soda Music or another new entrant is durably capturing younger users at TME's expense
- Any sign that Tencent-related-party dealings are extracting value at minority shareholders' expense

**Current stance: Monitor**

The valuation is statistically attractive (net-cash balance sheet, single-digit forward P/E, rising dividend, sell-side targets implying ~85% upside), which argues against dismissing TME outright. But the core operating trend (MAU decline) is unresolved, the company's own risk disclosures do not name what external reporting suggests is the most significant emerging competitive threat, and management has just reduced the market's ability to monitor the trend in real time. This combination — cheap, but on a metric the company has made harder to verify — argues for watching the next 1-2 quarters of company commentary and the FY2026 20-F rather than initiating a position now.

**Key triggers for position changes:**
- Resumption of quarterly (or any more frequent than annual) MAU/paying-user disclosure showing stabilization → upgrade toward Constructive
- Confirmed acceleration of MAU decline or explicit management acknowledgment of Soda Music-style competitive share loss → downgrade toward Avoid
- Adverse structural outcome in the NetEase antitrust litigation → downgrade toward Avoid
