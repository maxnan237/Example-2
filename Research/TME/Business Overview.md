# Tencent Music Entertainment Group (TME) — Business Overview

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02 | Primary source: Form 20-F (fiscal year ended December 31, 2025, filed April 17, 2026)*

---

## 1. What the Company Does

TME is China's largest online music entertainment platform by MAU, operating an integrated ecosystem of music streaming, karaoke/social entertainment, and long-form audio apps. It is a Cayman Islands holding company whose China operations are conducted through a VIE (variable interest entity) contractual structure, not direct equity ownership (see Section 6, Key Dependencies).

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "We are pioneering the way people enjoy online music, and over the years has grown into an integrated platform which offers a wide range of music-centric services, including social entertainment services, concerts and artist merchandise... We are the largest online music entertainment platform in China in terms of MAU as of December 31, 2025." → TME's core identity is a diversified, China-only music/audio ecosystem, not a pure streaming subscription service.

- **Employees:** 5,690 full-time (as of Dec 31, 2025) [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 6.D]
- **HQ:** China (VIE-structured; Cayman Islands incorporated holding company)
- **Founded/History:** Predecessor China Music Corporation renamed Tencent Music Entertainment Group in December 2016, following Tencent's 2016 acquisition of control [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.A]
- **Listings:** NYSE (ADS, "TME") since December 12, 2018; secondary listing on HKEX (stock code 1698) since September 2022
- **Fiscal year end:** December 31

## 2. Business Segments

TME discloses two revenue lines but does not report formal IFRS 8 segments:

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Notes to Financial Statements, Note 2.5 Segment reporting]: "For the purpose of internal reporting and management's operation review, the chief operating decision-makers and management personnel do not segregate the Group's business by product or service lines. Hence, the Group has only one operating segment." → No segment-level margin disclosure exists; only consolidated gross/operating margin is reported.

### Revenue Line 1 — Online Music Services

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "Our revenues generated from online music services were RMB17,325 million, RMB21,742 million and RMB26,726 million (US$3,822 million), accounting for 62.4%, 76.6% and 81.2% of our revenues in 2023, 2024 and 2025, respectively."

| RMB million | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 17,325 | 21,742 | 26,726 (US$3,822m) |
| % of total revenue | 62.4% | 76.6% | 81.2% |

### Revenue Line 2 — Social Entertainment Services and Others

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "Our revenues generated from social entertainment services and others were RMB10,427 million, RMB6,659 million and RMB6,176 million (US$883 million), accounting for 37.6%, 23.4% and 18.8% of our revenues in 2023, 2024 and 2025, respectively."

| RMB million | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 10,427 | 6,659 | 6,176 (US$883m) |
| % of total revenue | 37.6% | 23.4% | 18.8% |

The mix shift is structural: online music has gone from under two-thirds to over four-fifths of revenue in two years, while social entertainment (karaoke/live-streaming) revenue has fallen ~41% in RMB terms since 2023, reflecting tighter live-streaming compliance and reduced interactivity monetization industry-wide.

## 3. Revenue Model

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B — Music Subscription]: "We have built a multi-tiered membership, including ads membership, standard membership, and SVIP membership... users subscribing for our SVIP packages are offered various additional features and privileges, such as personalized app themes and avatar designs, Starlight cards, DTS sound effects, higher sound quality... as well as the Vocal Switch feature."

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "Benefiting from deepened collaborations with music labels, artists and the rollout of new, high-valued benefits, the number of SVIP subscribers exceeded 20 million as of December 31, 2025."

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B — Advertising Services]: "Our advertising offerings mainly include full-screen display ads that automatically appear when a user opens our mobile apps, industry standard banner ads... Additionally, we offer an ad-supported mode."

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B — Social Entertainment Services]: "We generate revenues from online karaoke and live streaming services primarily from sales and consumption of virtual gifts... Revenues generated on our platform are shared with our karaoke singers and live streaming performers or their agents, typically based on a percentage of the revenue generated from the sales of virtual gifts attributable to their performance."

### Revenue by type

| Stream | FY2025 | % of Total |
|---|---|---|
| Music subscriptions | RMB17,660m (US$2,525m) | 53.7% |
| Other online music (advertising, IP/merch/live events) | RMB9,066m | 27.6% |

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5 MD&A]: "Our revenues generated from music subscriptions increased by 16.0% from RMB15,227 million in 2024 to RMB17,660 million (US$2,525 million) in 2025." Subscription growth is the primary revenue driver of the business.

Not found in available filings: an exact split of the RMB9,066m residual online-music revenue between advertising and IP/merch/live-events sub-lines.

## 4. Customer Base & Concentration

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 5. Operating and Financial Review, Operating Metrics table]: "MAUs (in millions): 589 [2023] / 570 [2024] / 547 [2025]. Paying users (in millions): 100.9 / 117.6 / 125.1. Paying ratio: 17.1% / 20.6% / 22.9%. Monthly ARPPU (RMB): 10.0 / 10.8 / 11.8."

This is the central tension in TME's user metrics: MAUs have declined 7.1% over two years (589m → 547m), while paying users have risen 24% and ARPPU 18% over the same period — a "fewer but higher-value users" trend.

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "In the fourth quarter of 2025, the number of paying users for our online music services has reached 127.4 million." → Exit-quarter paying users above the FY2025 annual average.

**Customer concentration:** Not found in available filings. No single customer, advertiser, or counterparty is disclosed as material — consistent with a mass-consumer subscription/advertising business rather than a concentrated B2B model.

## 6. Key Dependencies

1. **Non-exclusive content licensing.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B — Content Sourcing Arrangements]: "Our licensing agreements with music labels and music copyright owners are on a non-exclusive basis." TME's core content supply — its entire raison d'être — can also be licensed by competitors such as NetEase Cloud Music. This is a dependency and a moat weakness simultaneously (see [[Moat Analysis]]).

2. **VIE contractual structure, not direct ownership.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.C]: "the Group primarily conducts its business in China through the VIEs and their respective subsidiaries in the PRC, based on a series of contractual arrangements... These contractual arrangements may not be as effective as direct ownership in providing us with control over the VIEs." ADS holders' economic claim on TME's licensed operating entities (Guangzhou Kugou, Beijing Kuwo, etc.) runs through contract, not equity — a structural dependency on continued PRC regulatory tolerance of the VIE model.

3. **Tencent Holdings — controlling shareholder and ecosystem partner.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D. Risk Factors]: "If we are no longer able to benefit from our business cooperation with Tencent, our business may be adversely affected." Distribution (Weixin/QQ login integration), cross-promotion (Tencent Games/Video), and capital-markets credibility are all tied to the Tencent relationship. [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: users "need to log into QQ Music, Kugou Music and Kuwo Music, which requires a Weixin or QQ account, or a mobile phone number."

4. **PRC regulatory licensing regime.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: TME's operating subsidiaries and VIEs hold Value-Added Telecommunication Business Operating Licenses, AVSP licenses, ICO Licenses, and Commercial Performance Licenses with fixed expiration dates (e.g., Guangzhou Kugou's core VATS license expires March 3, 2031). Loss or non-renewal of any of these would directly impair the ability to operate.

5. **Active antitrust litigation from principal domestic competitor.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 8. Legal Proceedings]: "In June 2024, NetEase Cloud Music Inc... filed a lawsuit... asserting claims of abuse of market dominance in relation to our music licensing practices... We are currently unable to estimate the potential loss, if any." Unresolved as of the FY2025 filing date.
