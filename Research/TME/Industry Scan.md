## 1. Market Size and Growth

**China online music/audio entertainment market:**

(Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) China's digital/streaming music market generated $3.9B in revenue in 2024, the 5th-largest recorded-music market globally. Growth projections vary meaningfully by provider: Statista projects ~4.56% CAGR (2024–2027) to $5.19B, or 5.58% CAGR (2024–2029) to $4.71B; Grand View Research projects a much higher 13.6% CAGR (2025–2030) specifically for China music streaming. User penetration is projected to rise from 21.7% (2024) to 26.1% (2029), reaching 372.7M users by 2029. The wide dispersion in growth estimates across providers reflects differing market-definition methodologies and should be treated as directional, not precise.

**Global music streaming market:** (Note: from external source) Estimates also vary significantly — Grand View Research: ~$62.5B in 2026, growing at 14.2% CAGR (2026–2033) to $158.1B; Coherent Market Insights: ~$58.70B in 2026. TME does not compete meaningfully outside China (see [[Business Overview]], Section 5), so global figures are context only, not a direct addressable-market read for TME.

TME's own filings do not provide independent third-party market-size estimates. Not found in available filings: a company-disclosed TAM figure.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

[TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B — Competition]: "We primarily compete with other online music and audio entertainment providers in China for users' time and attention. Additionally, we face broader competition from various online content offerings, including long- and short-form videos, karaoke services, live streaming, radio services, literature, and games provided by other online service providers." → TME frames its competitive set broadly (all China attention-economy content), not narrowly as "music streaming apps." No named competitor appears in this generic disclosure.

**NetEase Cloud Music — named competitor, active litigant.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 8. Financial Information — Legal Proceedings]: "In June 2024, NetEase Cloud Music Inc., together with some of its affiliates... filed a lawsuit in the Zhejiang Provincial Higher People's Court against us and some of our affiliates, asserting claims of abuse of market dominance in relation to our music licensing practices in the Chinese market... NetEase claims a substantial amount of damages and certain injunctive relief... the case is pending... We are currently unable to estimate the potential loss, if any." This lawsuit disclosure is new to the FY2025 filing — "NetEase" does not appear in the FY2023 or FY2024 20-Fs — indicating an escalating, unresolved competitive/legal dynamic.

(Note: from external source) NetEase Cloud Music (9899.HK) traded at HK$101.30 as of June 28, 2026 with a trailing P/E of ~8.37x — a meaningfully cheaper multiple than TME, though direct comparability is limited by differing business mixes.

**ByteDance / "Soda Music" — external-source competitive threat, not disclosed in filings.** (Note: from external source) Coverage of TME's March 17, 2026 earnings reaction attributes a 5% YoY MAU decline (to a reported 528M) to competition from ByteDance's Soda Music app, which reportedly grew to 140M+ MAU via a copyright-bundling strategy tied to Douyin licensing deals. Not found in available filings — ByteDance, Douyin, and Soda Music are not named anywhere in the FY2025, FY2024, or FY2023 20-F text. This is a material gap between what TME discloses to the SEC and what the market is actually pricing as the dominant competitive threat — flagged explicitly for the Bear Case.

**Spotify — cross-shareholder, not a China competitor.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 7. Major Shareholders]: Spotify AB beneficially owns 282,830,698 Class A ordinary shares (9.0% of total ordinary shares), with voting power over those shares assigned to Tencent under a 2017 share-swap Investor Agreement. Spotify does not operate in China; this is a balance-sheet/cap-table relationship, not competitive exposure.

**Named international competitors (Apple Music, YouTube Music):** Not found in available filings.

## 3. Regulatory Environment

TME operates in one of the most heavily regulated content/data sectors in China. Key regulatory threads from the filing:

**Licensing regime.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: TME's PRC subsidiaries and VIEs hold Value-Added Telecommunication Business Operating Licenses (VATS), AVSP licenses, ICO Licenses, "License for Production and Operation of Radio and TV Programs," and Commercial Performance Licenses, each with fixed renewal dates (e.g., Guangzhou Kugou's core VATS license expires March 3, 2031; Beijing Kuwo's AVSP expires January 29, 2028).

**Content regulation.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D. Risk Factors]: "China's internet, music entertainment and long-form audio industries are extensively regulated. Our failure to obtain and maintain requisite licenses or permits or to respond to any changes in policies, laws or regulations may materially and adversely impact our business." Named regulators: MOFCOM, Ministry of Culture and Tourism, National Copyright Administration, MIIT, NRTA, CAC. [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "we have from time to time been requested by relevant regulatory authorities to remove or restrict certain content that is deemed inappropriate or sensitive. Although we have not been materially penalized for our content so far..."

**Data security / cross-border listing review.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "We require that personal information and data we collect in China be stored and preserved within China... we, our PRC subsidiaries and the VIEs may be required to obtain permissions from the CSRC, and may be required to go through cybersecurity review by the CAC, in connection with any future offering and listing in an overseas market. As of the date of this annual report, we have not been subject to any cybersecurity review made by the CAC."

**Anti-monopoly.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D]: "The Anti-Monopoly Law of the PRC requires that the anti-monopoly law enforcement agency be notified in advance of any transaction where the parties' turnover in the China market and/or global market exceed certain thresholds." This is not theoretical — TME's music-licensing practices are the direct subject of the pending NetEase antitrust suit above, and the Ximalaya acquisition required SAMR (China's antitrust regulator) approval.

**Ximalaya deal conditions (subsequent event).** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.A]: TME announced a proposed Ximalaya Inc. acquisition (June 10, 2025) for cash of US$1.26 billion plus Class A shares of up to ~5.57% of TME's total outstanding ordinary shares, subject to regulatory approval — not yet closed as of the FY2025 20-F. (Note: from external source) SAMR granted conditional approval in May 2026 with binding conditions: no price increases, maintenance of free-content levels, and a prohibition on new exclusive audio licensing/copyright agreements — a regulator-imposed constraint directly on TME's content-licensing moat lever, post-close. (Note: from external source) The deal closed May 18, 2026, per StockTitan/Discoperi coverage — after the FY2025 20-F was filed, so this closing is not reflected in the audited financials reviewed for this report.

**HFCAA / PCAOB delisting risk.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D]: "Trading in our securities may be prohibited under the HFCAA and U.S. national securities exchanges, such as the NYSE, may determine to delist our securities if the PCAOB determines that it is unable to inspect or investigate completely our auditor for two consecutive years." TME's September 2022 HKEX secondary listing (code 1698, ADS-fungible with HK shares) partially mitigates the tail risk of a full NYSE delisting.

**AI regulation.** [TME_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 4.B]: "We are subject to risks and uncertainties associated with the evolving AI regulations, potential infringement claims and increasing costs for regulatory compliance... AI algorithms may be flawed, and the data used could be incomplete or biased." Relevant given TME's embedding of Tencent Yuanbao (AI assistant) into its products.

## 4. Industry Structural Notes

- TME does not report or benchmark itself against any independent industry data source in its filings (no cited IFPI, Statista, or similar third-party figures) — all market-size context in this document is external and flagged accordingly.
- The China music-streaming industry is effectively a duopoly-plus dynamic: TME (largest by MAU) and NetEase Cloud Music are the two major standalone players, with ByteDance's Soda Music emerging as a well-capitalized new entrant leveraging Douyin's short-video distribution — a competitive vector TME does not name in its own risk disclosures (see Section 2 above).
- Regulatory risk in this industry is not generic boilerplate — it is live and specific to TME (NetEase antitrust suit, Ximalaya SAMR conditions banning new exclusive licensing deals), meaning regulatory outcomes are actively shaping TME's competitive toolkit in real time, not just a background risk factor.
