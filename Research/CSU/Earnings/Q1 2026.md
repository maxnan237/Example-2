## Guidance vs. Reality

Both revenue and EPS beat consensus estimates. The net income growth figure (+170% YoY) should be read with real caution given [[Financial Deep Dive]]'s finding that CSU's reported net income has been meaningfully affected by large, non-operating revaluation items in multiple recent years (FY2023: $597M redeemable preferred securities charge; FY2025: $700M combined IRGA/Asseco revaluation charges) — it is plausible that Q1 2025's unusually low 5.1% net margin (the comparison base for this +170% growth figure) reflected a similar one-time charge, which would make the headline growth rate look better than the underlying operating trend actually improved. This should be verified directly against CSU's Q1 2026 financial statements (not reviewed in this research package) before treating the 170% figure as a clean operating metric.

**Acquisition pace reacceleration is the most important data point in this release.** Combined Q1 closed ($809M) and Q2 committed ($786M) acquisition spend totals approximately $1.6 billion — described externally as the most CSU has ever deployed in a single Q1 period. This is directly relevant to the central open question in [[Bear Case]] and [[Decision Log]]: whether the FY2023-FY2025 decline in annual acquisition cash spend ($1,609M→$1,347M→$1,227M) was opportunistic timing or a structural pipeline slowdown. This single data point suggests reacceleration, but should be confirmed across subsequent quarters before treating the multi-year trend as reversed.

**Organic growth rate:** not found in available external search as a standalone disclosed figure for Q1 2026 specifically (only the blended 20% revenue growth figure was found, which includes both organic and acquisition-driven growth).

## KPI Trends

Market reaction to the beat was muted-to-negative: (Note: from external source) the stock reportedly dipped ~0.4% after-hours despite the beat, with margin pressure attributed to recent acquisitions and higher professional-services costs — consistent with the pattern seen elsewhere in this research vault (e.g., MSFT) where strong headline results have not necessarily translated into positive stock reactions when investors are focused on a different underlying question (in CSU's case, likely the sustainability of the acquisition pace and the leadership transition, not near-term growth).

## What Changed

1. **Acquisition spend showed its strongest reacceleration in the data reviewed for this research package** — a potential turning point in the three-year decline documented in [[Financial Deep Dive]] and central to [[Bear Case]], though only one quarter's evidence.
2. **Net margin more than doubled YoY (5.1%→12%)** — directionally positive, but likely partly an artifact of comparing against a Q1 2025 base that may itself have included a one-time charge (unconfirmed).
3. **FCFA2S growth (+44% YoY) outpaced operating cash flow growth (+9% YoY)** — worth understanding why, given FCFA2S is a company-defined metric with several deductions (interest, IRGA charge, capex); this divergence is not explained in the external sources reviewed.
4. This is the first quarter reported under new CEO Mark Miller's full leadership (appointed September 2025) — no major strategic shifts were reported in the sources reviewed, consistent with a smooth continuation of the existing capital-allocation approach so far.
