**New CEO: Mark Miller.** (Note: from external source) Appointed CEO in September 2025; also President of Constellation and Executive Chairman of Volaris Group. Mathematics from McMaster University; Executive Marketing Program, Ivey Business School.

**This leadership transition is the single most important item to monitor for this investment thesis.** Mark Leonard is widely credited (externally) as the architect of CSU's decentralized capital-allocation philosophy and its unusually disciplined acquisition culture. Not found in available filings: his annual shareholder letters, which external commentary describes as central to understanding CSU's investment philosophy, do not appear in any of the four documents reviewed for this report — a genuine information gap for this research package specifically, though the letters are understood to be publicly available outside the scope of the documents pulled here.

## 2. Compensation

(Note: from external source, inpractise.com reference-check summary) Mark Miller has voluntarily waived his entitlement to salary and bonus — consistent with CSU's historically unusual, low-cash-compensation/high-ownership executive culture. Not found in available filings: this research package did not extract CSU's formal compensation disclosure (Statement of Executive Compensation, typically filed as part of the Management Information Circular) directly — the external-source note above should be verified against that primary document if precision on compensation structure is required.

The forced equity-reinvestment scheme described in [[Business Overview]] (senior employees required to reinvest at least 25% of incentive compensation into CSU shares, held 3-10 years; senior executives required to invest 75% of bonus in shares) is a structural, filed feature of the company's broader compensation philosophy, distinct from Miller's personal salary/bonus waiver.

## 3. Capital Allocation

**Acquisitions remain the dominant capital-allocation lever, though spend has trended down.** Cash paid for acquisitions: $1,609M (FY2023) → $1,347M (FY2024) → $1,227M (FY2025) [[Financial Deep Dive]] — a declining trend over three years even as operating cash flow grew from $1,779M to $2,732M over the same period. (Note: from external source) Q1 2026 shows a reacceleration: $809M of acquisitions closed in the quarter plus $786M committed for Q2, totaling ~$1.6 billion — described externally as the most CSU has ever deployed in a single Q1. This suggests the FY2023-FY2025 decline may reflect opportunistic timing rather than a structural slowdown in target availability, though this is inferred from a single subsequent data point, not confirmed in the audited financial statements reviewed.

**No share buybacks; static share count.** [[Financial Deep Dive]]: shares outstanding were unchanged at 21,191,530 between Dec 31, 2024 and Dec 31, 2025 — CSU does not repurchase shares, a deliberate capital-allocation choice consistent with prioritizing acquisitions over financial engineering.

**Dividend is a token capital return, not a primary lever.** [[Financial Deep Dive]]: $1.00/share quarterly ($4.00/year), unchanged FY2024→FY2025, totaling only $85M annually against FCFA2S of $1,683M (FY2025) — the overwhelming majority of free cash flow is redeployed into acquisitions, not returned to shareholders directly.

**Debt issuance funds growth without diluting shareholders.** [[Financial Deep Dive]]: CSU issued $1,000M of Senior Notes in February 2024 (2029/2034 maturities) and has drawn on various subsidiary-level, non-recourse debt facilities — a disciplined approach to using leverage (much of it ring-fenced at the subsidiary level, without recourse to the parent) to fund acquisitions rather than issuing new equity, which would dilute the static, famously small share count.

## 5. Guidance Credibility

CSU does not appear to issue formal forward financial guidance (consistent with the qualitative MD&A commentary style typical of the company) — not found in available filings. The most useful forward-looking, checkable disclosures are the $7.7 billion contracted-not-yet-recognized revenue backlog (~61% expected within 12 months) and the quarterly organic-growth-rate trend, both of which can be tracked against actual results each quarter.

(Note: from external source) Q1 2026 results reportedly beat consensus on both EPS ($27.37 vs. $25.28 estimate) and revenue ($3.18B vs. $3.14B estimate), with net income up 170% YoY and free cash flow available to shareholders up 44% YoY — a strong quarter, though as with the FY2023/FY2025 annual results ([[Financial Deep Dive]]), some of this variance likely reflects the same kind of large, non-operating revaluation items that have made CSU's year-over-year net income comparisons volatile; this should be verified against the actual Q1 2026 financial statements before treating the headline growth rate as a clean operating metric.

## 6. Overall Assessment

CSU's management culture — decentralized capital allocation, forced equity reinvestment for senior managers, minimal cash compensation for the top executive, no share buybacks, and a static share count funded by disciplined debt issuance — reflects a genuinely unusual and, by its long track record, effective approach to running a serial acquirer. The near-term risk is not a change in philosophy but a change in personnel: Mark Leonard's departure from the Board (effective May 2026) removes the founder who is externally credited with designing this entire system, even though he remains involved in an advisory capacity. Mark Miller's 30+ year tenure within the CSU/Volaris ecosystem (including having been the very first acquisition target in 1995) is a credible succession signal — this is not an outside hire unfamiliar with the culture — but any investor in CSU should treat the next several years of capital-allocation discipline and acquisition pace as the key test of whether the system Leonard built is truly durable beyond his direct involvement, or whether it required his specific judgment to sustain the historical hurdle-rate discipline.
