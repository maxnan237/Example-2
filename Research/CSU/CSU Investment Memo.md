# CSU Investment Memo

**Constellation Software Inc. (TSX: CSU) | 2026-07-02**

*Note on sourcing: Constellation Software does not file with the U.S. SEC. This research package is built from the company's own Annual Information Form and audited financial statements/MD&A, sourced directly from its investor-relations site (SEDAR+ disclosure), not SEC EDGAR.*

---

## The Business in 30 Seconds

Constellation Software is a Canadian serial acquirer of mission-critical vertical market software (VMS) businesses — six operating groups, 150+ distinct verticals, no customer over 2% of revenue, and ~75% recurring maintenance revenue — run on a deliberately decentralized capital-allocation model with an IRR hurdle rate for every acquisition [CSU_AIF_FY2024.pdf, pages 13-15].

---

## What's Going Right

**Recurring revenue continues rising as a share of the mix.** Maintenance and other recurring revenue grew from 73.5% (FY2024) to 74.8% (FY2025) of total revenue [CSU_Q4FY2025_MDA.pdf, page 4], the clearest evidence of durable switching-cost protection strengthening, not weakening.

**Organic growth reaccelerated through FY2025.** Unadjusted quarterly organic growth went from 0% (Q1 2025) to 6% (Q4 2025) [CSU_Q4FY2025_MDA.pdf, page 4].

**Static share count, no dilution.** Shares outstanding were unchanged at 21,191,530 between Dec 2024 and Dec 2025 [CSU_Q4FY2025_FinancialStatements.pdf, Note 17] — CSU funds growth via cash flow and disciplined debt, not shareholder dilution.

**Substantial forward revenue visibility.** $7.7 billion of contracted-not-yet-recognized revenue as of Dec 31, 2025, ~61% expected within 12 months [CSU_Q4FY2025_MDA.pdf, page 22].

**Q1 2026 results beat consensus with a reported acquisition-spend reacceleration.** (Note: from external source) Revenue +20% YoY, EPS beat, and ~$1.6 billion of Q1/Q2 2026 combined acquisition deployment — the strongest Q1 acquisition pace in the company's reported history.

---

## What Could Go Wrong

**Acquisition cash spend declined for three consecutive years.** $1,609M (FY2023) → $1,347M (FY2024) → $1,227M (FY2025) [CSU_Q4FY2025_FinancialStatements.pdf] — even as operating cash flow grew from $1,779M to $2,732M over the same period. This is CSU's core growth engine, and it has been shrinking in absolute dollars.

**Founder Mark Leonard is departing the Board.** (Note: from external source) Effective after the May 15, 2026 AGM, with new CEO Mark Miller (appointed September 2025) taking over full leadership — a genuine succession-risk event for a company whose capital-allocation culture is externally credited almost entirely to Leonard's design.

**Reported net income is unusually volatile due to large, recurring non-operating items.** FY2023 was suppressed by a $597M redeemable preferred securities charge; FY2025 by a combined $700M IRGA/Asseco revaluation charge [CSU_Q4FY2025_FinancialStatements.pdf] — headline EPS and trailing P/E are not reliable year-over-year comparison tools without adjustment.

**No segment-level financial disclosure.** CSU aggregates all six operating groups into a single IFRS 8 reportable segment [CSU_Q4FY2025_FinancialStatements.pdf, Note 23] — investors cannot independently verify which operating groups are driving growth or margin trends.

**Rising acquisition-market competition.** (Note: from external source) Private equity and other strategic roll-ups are increasingly competing for the same small, niche VMS targets CSU has historically acquired at disciplined valuations.

**FX volatility.** Only ~49% of revenue is USD-denominated against a 100% USD reporting currency — the FX line swung from a $26M gain (FY2024) to a $154M loss (FY2025) [CSU_Q4FY2025_FinancialStatements.pdf].

## Decision

**Current stance: Monitor**

Constellation Software's underlying portfolio quality — hundreds of switching-cost-protected, niche mission-critical software businesses with rising recurring revenue and no meaningful customer concentration — is genuinely among the strongest in this research vault. The open questions are specific and checkable rather than existential: whether the three-year decline in acquisition cash spend was opportunistic timing (as the reported Q1/Q2 2026 reacceleration suggests) or a structural narrowing of the acquisition pipeline, and whether new CEO Mark Miller sustains the disciplined capital-allocation culture externally credited to founder Mark Leonard following his Board departure. The stock's sharp 52-week decline (down ~43.6% from its high) already reflects real market uncertainty on both fronts. This is a name to watch for confirmation over the next 1-2 quarters and the FY2026 annual filing, rather than either a clear buy or a clear avoid at the current price.

**Key triggers to watch:**
- FY2026 annual results (expected ~March 2027) — does the acquisition-spend reacceleration reported for Q1/Q2 2026 hold for the full year?
- Mark Miller's first full year of public capital-allocation track record and commentary
- Continued organic growth trend in quarterly disclosures
- Normalization of the large non-operating revaluation items affecting reported EPS

**Key risks to monitor:**
- A structurally shrinking acquisition pipeline despite ample available cash flow
- Leadership-transition-driven drift in capital-allocation discipline
- Rising competition from private equity and other roll-ups compressing acquisition economics
- AI-driven competitive disruption at the individual portfolio-company level (a newly-disclosed risk factor as of the FY2025 MD&A)
