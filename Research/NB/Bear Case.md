# NioCorp Developments Ltd. (NB) — Bear Case

*Analysis date: 2026-08-04*

This document states the bear case in its strongest, most genuine form — not softened for balance. NioCorp's entire investment thesis rests on a chain of not-yet-completed events (financing, permitting, construction, ramp-up, commercial production at a novel processing facility), each of which has meaningful, filing-documented failure probability.

---

## 1. Thesis Killer

**The project may never get built, or may get built only after so much additional dilution that today's shareholders are left with a small fraction of the eventual value.** NioCorp is not a mining company — it is a pre-revenue permitting-and-financing vehicle for a project whose own audited financials describe "substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern" in all three fiscal years reviewed [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firms; NB_10K_FY2024.htm, same; NB_10K_FY2023.htm, Item 1A]. Nothing about the FY2025 cash improvement changes this qualification — management's own MD&A states the doubt "cannot be alleviated with confidence until the Company has entered into a business climate where funding of its planned ongoing operating activities is secured" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]. The thesis is not "will the deposit prove economic" (it likely is, directionally) — it is "will NioCorp specifically be the entity that successfully finances, builds, and operates it, on terms that leave meaningful value for current shareholders."

---

## 2. Structural Weaknesses

**Single-asset concentration.** Elk Creek is NioCorp's only material property. There is no diversification if permitting stalls, if the deposit underperforms the (stale) feasibility study, or if a discretionary Nebraska permit — particularly the Radioactive Materials License the company has been pursuing "since 2014" without resolution [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2] — is denied or indefinitely delayed. Notably, the company's own risk-factor summary does not include an explicit, standalone "single-project concentration risk" bullet [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A] — this risk is real but somewhat underemphasized in the company's own disclosure relative to its practical significance.

**Zero Proven reserves.** The entire 36,656 kt mineral reserve is classified Probable under S-K 1300 — there is no Proven-tier reserve at all [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2]. The FY2025 drilling campaign is explicitly aimed at upgrading this, meaning as of the most recent audited filing, the company's own reserve base sits at the lower-confidence tier a lender or partner would want to see before committing $800 million.

**Stale, undisclosed project economics.** The 2022 Feasibility Study's headline NPV ($2.8B) and IRR (29.2%) — the numbers that justify the entire valuation case — appear nowhere in the body text of any of the three 10-Ks reviewed for this report (confirmed via targeted search). The company itself is updating the study specifically because EXIM's due-diligence process requires it [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2], an implicit admission that the existing economics are not current enough to support the financing the company needs. There is real risk the updated study shows a worse NPV/IRR given four-plus years of capex and cost inflation across the mining sector broadly.

**Material weakness in internal controls.** As of June 30, 2025, management concluded disclosure controls and procedures were not effective due to material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 9A]. This is disclosed at the exact moment the company is attempting to present itself as a credible counterparty for $800 million in federal debt financing — a genuine governance concern, not a technicality.

**Heavy, continuing dilution.** Shares outstanding grew 54% in FY2025 alone (38.1M → 58.5M) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Consolidated Balance Sheets], and management's own filing states future financings "will likely be completed at a discount to the then-current market price... and will likely be dilutive to current shareholders" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7] — not a hedge, a direct forecast. External reporting indicates this continued at an even larger scale into FY2026 (share count roughly 2.5x from FYE2025 to March 2026) (Note: from external source, see [[Valuation Check]]). Combined warrants and options at FYE2025 (~33.0M) equal roughly 56% of shares outstanding — a large latent overhang even before further primary dilution.

**Non-exclusive competitive position against a dominant, well-capitalized incumbent.** CBMM supplies approximately 85% of the world's niobium, per NioCorp's own risk-factor disclosure, and the filing explicitly states CBMM's pricing behavior could adversely affect NioCorp's value [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A] — NioCorp would enter as a price-taker against a dominant, privately-held incumbent with no obligation to accommodate a new entrant.

**Only niobium is contracted; scandium and titanium are not.** All three disclosed offtake agreements (CMC Cometals, Traxys, ThyssenKrupp) relate to niobium [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 15]; the company's own filing lists completing offtake agreements for scandium and titanium as still-outstanding work [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2] — two of the project's three planned product lines have no disclosed committed buyer.

## 3. Assumptions at Risk

| Assumption in the bull/base case | What would break it |
|---|---|
| EXIM will ultimately approve ~$800M in debt financing | EXIM's own process has taken over two years without a final commitment as of the FY2025 filing; the company explicitly states "no assurances" of success [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]. Political/administrative changes at EXIM, or a negative outcome from the updated feasibility study, could stall or kill the application entirely. |
| The updated feasibility study will confirm or improve on 2022 economics | Global mining capex and labor costs have risen materially since 2019 (the constant-dollar basis of the 2022 study's cash-flow table); the update could reveal a smaller NPV, longer payback, or higher initial capex than the $1,141M figure currently used as the benchmark. |
| Remaining Nebraska permits (especially the Radioactive Materials License) will be granted in a reasonable timeframe | This specific permit has been pending in some form "since 2014" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2] — over a decade — with no disclosed resolution timeline. |
| Dilution will taper off once construction financing closes | Management's own language frames further dilution as a near-certainty, not a contingency, until financing is fully secured [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]. |
| The novel integrated hydromet/pyromet processing flow will perform at commercial scale as designed | This flow has not been proven at commercial scale (this project would be a first-of-its-kind integrated Nb/Sc/Ti processing facility); technical/ramp-up risk at first-of-kind processing facilities is a well-documented failure mode across the mining industry generally (industry-pattern observation, not filing-specific). |

## 4. Permanent Impairment / Total-Loss Scenarios

1. **Financing failure cascade:** EXIM ultimately declines or indefinitely delays the application, the updated feasibility study shows materially worse economics, and equity markets close to further dilutive raises during a period of broader risk-off sentiment in small-cap miners — leaving NioCorp unable to fund construction and forced to either sell/joint-venture the project at a discount or wind down. Given the going-concern qualification has persisted for three straight fiscal years already, this is not a remote tail scenario; it is the base case the auditors are formally flagging every year.
2. **Permitting denial:** the Radioactive Materials License (or another discretionary Nebraska permit) is ultimately denied rather than merely delayed, which would likely require a fundamental redesign of the processing flow or render the project uneconomic — the company provides no contingency plan for this outcome in the filings reviewed.
3. **Commodity price collapse:** a sustained decline in niobium, scandium, or titanium prices (e.g., if CBMM aggressively defends share by cutting price, or if Chinese scandium/titanium supply expands) could push the (already-stale) project economics below the threshold needed to justify the ~$1,141M capex commitment, at which point financing sources would likely walk away regardless of permitting/technical progress.
4. **Dilution to irrelevance:** even absent an outright failure, a scenario in which the company must repeatedly issue equity at depressed prices over several more years to bridge financing gaps could leave any single cohort of current shareholders with a heavily diluted claim on eventual value — a "long, slow bleed" outcome distinct from outright failure but similarly damaging to a position established at today's price.

## 5. What Would Prove the Bear Right

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| EXIM application stalls beyond FY2026 without a final commitment, or is formally declined | Confirms the primary financing pillar is not viable on the timeline/terms the market is currently pricing |
| Updated feasibility study (in progress since April 2025) shows a materially lower NPV or higher capex than the 2022 study | Would directly undercut the only external NAV anchor the market has used to value the stock |
| Continued going-concern qualification in the FY2026 10-K despite the large external-reported cash balance | Would confirm auditors still see the capital gap as structurally unresolved, not just a timing issue |
| Radioactive Materials License remains unresolved past another multi-year mark | Confirms the decade-plus permitting delay is not close to resolution |
| Further material weaknesses disclosed, or an auditor change | Would deepen concerns about the company's readiness for institutional-scale debt financing |
| Share count continues to grow at a similar pace (>50%/year) without a corresponding construction-financing close | Confirms dilution is structural, not a one-time bridge to a near-term catalyst |

None of these signals require a catastrophic external event — they are simply the continuation of patterns already visible in the three fiscal years of filings reviewed for this report. The bear case for NioCorp is less "something unexpected goes wrong" and more "the same unresolved issues that have persisted for three straight years continue to persist."
