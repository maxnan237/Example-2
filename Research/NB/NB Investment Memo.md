# NB Investment Memo

**NioCorp Developments Ltd. (Nasdaq: NB) | 2026-08-04**

---

## The Business in 30 Seconds

NioCorp is a British Columbia-incorporated, Colorado-headquartered, pre-revenue development-stage company whose entire value proposition is the Elk Creek Project in Johnson County, Nebraska — a carbonatite deposit that would produce niobium, scandium, and titanium dioxide (plus a potential rare-earth by-product) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1]. It is not yet under construction, has zero Proven reserves (its entire reserve base is Probable-tier), and generates no revenue [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]. The company is a U.S. SEC 10-K filer with a fiscal year ending June 30.

---

## What's Going Right

**The balance sheet has been transformed.** Cash grew from $2.0 million (FYE2024) to $25.6 million (FYE2025) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7], and — per external reporting not yet reflected in an audited filing — to approximately $419.2 million by March 31, 2026 [[Earnings/Q3 FY2026 Update]]. All debt (Yorkville Convertible Debentures, April 2024 Notes, the Smith Loan) was fully retired during FY2025, leaving the company debt-free [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 8].

**Three confirmed niobium offtake agreements** provide commercial validation for one of the project's three planned products: CMC Cometals (2016), Traxys North America (as assignee, 2020), and ThyssenKrupp Metallurgical Products GmbH [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 15].

**Government financing relationships are advancing, though incomplete.** The EXIM Bank application (up to $800 million) has cleared multiple formal review steps since June 2023 and sits at step two of four as of the FY2025 filing [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]; a DoD-affiliated reimbursement agreement (up to $10.0 million, milestone-based) was signed August 4, 2025 [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 17].

**A genuinely scarce resource.** CBMM (Brazil) supplies ~85% of the world's niobium, and there are currently zero primary scandium mines anywhere in the world [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A] — if built, Elk Creek would be one of very few non-CBMM niobium sources and among the first primary scandium producers globally, in a policy environment actively favoring Western critical-minerals diversification (Note: from external source, see [[Industry Scan]]).

**External reporting suggests physical construction may have begun.** Mine portal construction activity was reportedly announced in February 2026 (Note: from external source, not yet independently verified against a primary filing — see [[Earnings/Q3 FY2026 Update]]).

---

## What Could Go Wrong

**Going-concern doubt has persisted, unresolved, for three straight audited fiscal years.** [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firms]: "These conditions raise substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern" — nearly identical language appears in the FY2024 and FY2023 filings. Management's own MD&A states this cannot be resolved until construction financing is actually secured [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7], meaning even the dramatically improved FY2026 cash position (per external reporting) does not, by the company's own logic, resolve the underlying doubt.

**Project economics disclosed in the 10-Ks are stale, and the headline NPV/IRR figures don't even appear in the filings.** The only NAV anchor — a $2.8 billion pre-tax NPV and 29.2% pre-tax IRR — comes from a June 2022 press release, not from any of the three audited 10-Ks reviewed, which disclose only the underlying LOM cash-flow table without a summary NPV/IRR [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2]. Management has launched a feasibility-study update specifically because EXIM's diligence requires current figures — an implicit admission the 2022 numbers are dated.

**Aggressive, company-acknowledged dilution.** Shares outstanding grew 54% in FY2025 (38.1M → 58.5M) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Consolidated Balance Sheets], and management's own filing states future financings "will likely be dilutive to current shareholders" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7] — not a hedge, a forecast. External reporting shows this continued at an even larger scale into FY2026 (~2.5x share count growth in under 12 months) [[Valuation Check]].

**Material weakness in internal control over financial reporting**, disclosed as of the FY2025 fiscal year-end [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 9A] — a governance concern at precisely the moment the company needs to present as a credible counterparty for $800 million in federal debt financing.

**A permit that has been pending for over a decade.** The Radioactive Materials License from Nebraska's NDHHS has been in process "since 2014" with no disclosed resolution timeline [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2].

**Two of three planned products remain uncontracted.** All disclosed offtake agreements relate to niobium; scandium and titanium offtake agreements are listed as still-outstanding work [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2].

## Decision

**Current stance: Speculative**

NioCorp is not a company to evaluate on conventional operating or valuation metrics — it is a financing-and-permitting option on a genuinely scarce critical-minerals deposit. The bull case is real: a dramatically improved balance sheet, three confirmed niobium offtake agreements, advancing (if incomplete) EXIM and DoD government relationships, and a policy backdrop that favors Western critical-minerals diversification. But the bear case is equally real and, unusually for this kind of memo, largely drawn directly from the company's own audited disclosures rather than external speculation: three consecutive years of unresolved going-concern doubt, a reserve base that is 100% Probable-tier with zero Proven reserves, project economics (NPV/IRR) that the company's own 10-Ks do not disclose, a disclosed material weakness in internal controls, and dilution that management itself forecasts will continue. This is a name for a small, speculative position sized for genuine binary-outcome risk — not a core holding — until the EXIM financing decision and the updated feasibility study both land, at which point the picture will be far clearer in either direction.

**Key triggers to watch:**
- EXIM Bank final financing decision (approval, decline, or continued indefinite delay)
- Publication of the updated Elk Creek Feasibility Study (in progress since April 2025)
- Resolution of the Radioactive Materials License (pending since 2014)
- Whether the FY2026 10-K (expected ~September 2026) removes or maintains the going-concern qualification
- Any scandium or titanium offtake agreements (currently absent)

**Key risks to monitor:**
- Continued dilution at a pace inconsistent with an approaching financing close
- Single-project concentration — there is no fallback asset if Elk Creek's financing or permitting path fails
- CBMM pricing behavior and broader niobium/scandium/titanium price trends
- Governance/controls quality given the disclosed FY2025 material weakness
