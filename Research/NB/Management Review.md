## 1. Leadership

**CEO:** Mark A. Smith — Chief Executive Officer, President, and Executive Chairman (Note: from external source, Fintool executive profile, 2026). Previously CEO of Molycorp, a prior U.S. rare-earths producer (Note: from external source, niocorp.com historical press release "Former Molycorp CEO Mark A. Smith Joins NioCorp Developments Ltd."). No evidence of a recent CEO change was found — Smith continues to lead as of mid-2026. His Molycorp background is directly relevant (prior experience building and operating a U.S. critical-minerals producer), though Molycorp itself filed for bankruptcy in 2015 during his tenure as CEO — a fact worth noting as color, though the specific circumstances of that outcome were not independently verified in this research and should not be assumed to reflect on Smith's current execution.

**Other named executives** (Note: from external source, Form 4 filing summaries via search, 2026): CFO Neal S. Shah; COO Scott Honan; Chief Communications Officer James T. Sims.

**Notable governance item — CEO as lender:** [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 8] discloses a "Smith Loan" — a September 11, 2024 non-revolving credit facility of up to $2.0 million extended by CEO Mark Smith to the company; $504K was drawn and fully repaid (including a $41K origination fee) within FY2025. A related-party loan from the sitting CEO to a going-concern-qualified company is a governance detail worth flagging explicitly — it demonstrates insider confidence/willingness to bridge liquidity, but related-party lending arrangements always warrant scrutiny of terms fairness. The exact interest rate/terms of the Smith Loan beyond the $41K origination fee were not extracted in detail — not found in available filings (beyond what is quoted above).

## 2. Capital Allocation Track Record

Management's stated funding strategy — roughly two-thirds debt (anchored by the EXIM application), one-third equity, to cover the ~$1,141 million initial capex [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7] — has not yet been executed as planned; to date, essentially all financing raised has been equity (plus now-fully-retired convertible debt), not the low-cost EXIM debt the plan assumes. This is not necessarily a management failure (EXIM's multi-step federal approval process is outside the company's full control), but it does mean the company has borne more dilution than its own stated plan implies, at least through the FY2025 fiscal year-end.

On the positive side: management successfully retired all convertible debt during FY2025 (Yorkville debentures, April 2024 Notes, and the Smith Loan all reduced to $0 by fiscal year-end) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 8], and grew cash from $2.0 million to $25.6 million over the same year — a genuine improvement in balance-sheet discipline and financing execution versus the company's FY2023-FY2024 history, when it was running with a working-capital deficit [NB_10K_FY2024.htm, Item 7].

Operating cost discipline: excluding the one-time FY2023 SPAC-transaction costs, underlying cash operating expenses (employee costs + professional fees + exploration) have generally trended down from FY2023 levels, even as the company ramped its FY2025 drilling program — consistent with a small, lean team (seven full-time employees, unchanged for three straight years) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1] managing spend carefully during a capital-constrained period.

## 4. Guidance Credibility

NioCorp does not provide traditional revenue/earnings guidance (it is pre-revenue), so the relevant credibility test is whether management's stated financing and construction timelines have held up. Evidence is mixed:

- **EXIM timeline:** application submitted June 2023; as of the FY2025 10-K (filed September 2025), the application remained at step two of a four-step process [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A] — over two years without a final commitment, though this reflects federal-agency pace as much as company execution.
- **Liquidity planning:** the FY2023 10-K projected a 12-month cash need of ~$11.8 million; by FY2025, the forward 12-month need had grown to $40-50 million [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7] — not a broken promise, but a sign that cost/capital estimates have scaled up meaningfully as the project has advanced toward construction-readiness.
- **Feasibility study currency:** management itself acknowledges the 2022 Feasibility Study needs updating for EXIM's due-diligence purposes [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2], implicitly conceding that the headline project economics investors have been using for several years are dated.
- (Note: from external source) CEO Mark Smith reportedly expressed confidence in January 2026 commentary that full project financing (EXIM debt + equity) could close by end of Q2 2026; whether that specific timeline was met was not independently confirmed in this research — flagged for follow-up in a future update.

## 5. Overall Assessment

NioCorp's management team, led by a CEO with direct prior experience running a U.S. critical-minerals producer, has made real, verifiable progress on the financing side over the FY2023-FY2025 period: the company eliminated all debt, grew cash more than tenfold, and advanced (though did not complete) both EXIM and DoD government-financing relationships. Three confirmed niobium offtake agreements provide some commercial validation. Set against this: the company disclosed a material weakness in internal control over financial reporting as of the FY2025 fiscal year-end — a real governance concern precisely when the company needs to present a credible, well-controlled financial reporting function to institutional lenders like EXIM — and shareholders have absorbed substantial, management-acknowledged-as-necessary dilution, with more explicitly expected. The related-party CEO loan, while fully repaid and relatively small, is a detail worth continued monitoring rather than a red flag on its own. On balance, management's execution has been directionally positive but the company remains fundamentally pre-financing, pre-construction, and dependent on federal-agency approval timelines outside its control.
