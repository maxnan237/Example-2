The jump from $25.6M (June 2025, audited/filed) to $419.2M (March 2026, per external reporting of the unaudited 10-Q) reflects a wave of equity financings not covered by the FY2025 10-K reviewed for this report (October 2025 registered direct offering, ~$150.2M gross at $9.34/share; February 2026 public offering, ~$100.0M gross at $5.00/share; plus other smaller raises) (Note: from external source, SEC filing-metadata summaries and niocorp.com press releases, 2025-2026). **This is a major, positive post-FY2025-filing development not reflected in the audited financial statements this report otherwise relies on** — cash position has grown roughly 16x since the last audited balance sheet date. Total liabilities were reported at $34.6 million and shareholders' equity at $435.4 million as of March 31, 2026 (Note: from external source, same).

- Debt: reported as $0 / debt-free as of the most recent external snapshots (Note: from external source, companiesmarketcap.com / Simply Wall St aggregation, 2026), consistent with the FY2025 10-K's fully-retired debt position.
- Implied enterprise value: ~$730M market cap − ~$419M cash − $0 debt ≈ ~$311M (Note: calculated from mismatched-date external data points above — the market cap snapshot is August 2026 while the cash snapshot is March 2026, so this EV figure is illustrative only, not contemporaneous).

**Important caveat on dilution behind this cash growth:** the company's own FY2025 10-K explicitly warns that further equity financing "will likely be completed at a discount to the then-current market price... and will likely be dilutive to current shareholders" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]. External reporting confirms this played out at scale: shares outstanding grew from 58,491,196 at FYE2025 to approximately 125,321,172 by February 6, 2026, and to ~145.3–145.6 million by March–May 2026 — roughly a **2.5x increase in share count in under 12 months** (Note: from external source, SEC filing-metadata summaries and niocorp.com press releases). The cash windfall and the dilution are two sides of the same coin.

## 3. Project NAV and P/NAV

**The only published project NAV is from the June 2022 Elk Creek Feasibility Study**: pre-tax NPV of $2.8 billion, pre-tax IRR of 29.2%, 38-year mine life (Note: from external source, company press release "NioCorp Announces Results of Positive Feasibility Study for its Elk Creek Superalloy Materials Project," niocorp.com, June 2022). As detailed in [[Business Overview]] and [[Financial Deep Dive]], **these specific NPV/IRR figures do not appear anywhere in the body text of the FY2023, FY2024, or FY2025 10-Ks** — the filings disclose the underlying LOM cash-flow table (gross revenue, operating costs, operating margin, taxes, operating cash flow, all at an 8% discount rate) but stop short of the summary NPV/IRR conclusion that the 2022 press release contained.

This NAV is now **more than four years old** and does not reflect current capex inflation, updated commodity price assumptions, or the financing structure actually being pursued (a mix of EXIM debt and equity, rather than the 100%-equity-basis assumption underlying the 2022 study's cash-flow table). Management has explicitly launched an updated feasibility study specifically because the existing economics are stale and insufficient for EXIM's due-diligence requirements [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2]; **no new NAV has been published as of the August 4, 2026 research date** (Note: from external source, Junior Mining Network / niocorp.com, engagement announced April 29, 2025, update not yet complete).

**Illustrative P/NAV:** ~$730M market cap ÷ $2.8B (stale 2022) NAV ≈ **0.26x**. This ratio should be treated with significant caution — it uses a four-year-old NAV against a current market cap that reflects a materially larger share count and a materially different (much stronger) balance sheet than existed when the NAV was calculated. A P/NAV below 1.0x is often read as "cheap" for a project developer, but that read is only as good as the NAV's currency, and this NAV is not current.

## 6. Scenario Framework

Given the absence of a current, filing-disclosed NPV, these scenarios are necessarily more qualitative than a standard operating-company framework and are explicitly (inferred from the stale 2022 feasibility study, the current unresolved financing status, and typical development-stage mining re-rating patterns) rather than derived from filed data.

### Bull Case
**Assumptions:** EXIM debt financing closes on favorable terms; updated feasibility study confirms or improves on 2022 economics despite capex inflation; construction proceeds on the ~45-month timeline with no major permitting delays (including the outstanding Radioactive Materials License); niobium/scandium prices hold or strengthen amid Western supply-chain diversification demand.

| Metric | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Construction financing fully closes within 12-18 months; project reaches production within the 2022 study's ~45-month build window from that point |
| Value driver | Market re-rates NioCorp from an option-on-a-project to a financed, de-risked developer — historically the single largest value inflection for miners in this stage is closing construction financing itself, independent of commodity prices |
| Qualitative fair value | Materially above current price — potentially multiples of the current ~$730M market cap, though a specific NPV-per-share figure cannot be responsibly quoted given the absence of an updated, filing-verified NPV |

### Base Case
**Assumptions:** EXIM process continues to progress but takes longer than management's most optimistic public commentary suggests; company continues to fund the gap via further equity dilution; updated feasibility study is published showing broadly similar (inflation-adjusted) economics to 2022; construction begins but on a delayed timeline relative to current expectations.

| Metric | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Continued incremental progress, continued dilution, financing closes but later/smaller than hoped |
| Value driver | Market assigns a discount for execution/timeline risk even as fundamentals slowly de-risk |
| Qualitative fair value | Roughly in line with to modestly above current market cap, with continued share-count growth offsetting NAV progress on a per-share basis |

### Bear Case
**Assumptions:** EXIM financing does not materialize on acceptable terms (a real risk given the filing's own "no assurances" language) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]; the Radioactive Materials License or another discretionary Nebraska permit is delayed or denied; the updated feasibility study reveals materially worse economics due to capex inflation; the company is forced into further, more dilutive equity raises or a construction delay/pause.

| Metric | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Project financing continues to slip; company remains perpetually "close" to full financing without closing it; going-concern language persists indefinitely |
| Value driver | Market re-rates toward a pure optionality/exploration-stage valuation, discounting the 2022 project economics heavily for both staleness and execution risk |
| Qualitative fair value | Materially below current market cap — a return toward the company's pre-FY2025 valuation range is plausible if the financing narrative stalls |

## Key Valuation Considerations

NioCorp's valuation cannot be reduced to a clean multiple or a confident NPV-per-share figure with the information available in its own SEC filings — the company's own 10-Ks do not disclose NPV/IRR, and the only external NAV figure (the June 2022 study's $2.8B pre-tax NPV) is now stale by the company's own admission (it commissioned an update specifically because this figure needs revisiting). What is verifiable is a dramatically improved balance sheet (debt-free, cash growing from $25.6M at FYE2025 to a reported $419.2M by March 2026 per external sources) purchased at the cost of substantial, ongoing shareholder dilution (share count roughly 2.5x in under a year). The investment case is best framed as a binary-ish bet on financing execution (EXIM debt closing, permitting completing, an updated feasibility study confirming economics) rather than a traditional multiple-based valuation exercise — see [[Bear Case]] for the specific ways this could go wrong, and [[Decision Log]] for concrete triggers to watch.
