# NioCorp Developments Ltd. (NB) — Industry Scan

*Analysis date: 2026-08-04*

## 1. Market Size and Growth

**Niobium:** (Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) The global niobium market is estimated at ~$4.1 billion in 2026, projected to reach ~$7.41 billion by 2035 (6.80% CAGR per one forecaster); other sources cite CAGRs ranging from 3.7% to 6.5% depending on methodology and forecast window (Note: from external source ResearchNester / 360ResearchReports / VerifiedMarketReports aggregation, 2026 market forecast reports). Batteries are cited as the fastest-growing niobium application, driven by charging-speed and cycle-life improvements in niobium-oxide anode chemistries.

**Scandium:** (Note: from external source) Estimates vary widely depending on product scope (scandium oxide vs. metal vs. chloride segments valued separately) — figures range from $222 million to just over $1 billion for 2026, with CAGR estimates ranging 5.8%–14.5% through 2031–2034 (Note: from external sources including Mordor Intelligence, Verified Market Reports, GII Research, 2026 reports). This dispersion reflects scandium's status as a small, illiquid, non-exchange-traded market with limited independent price discovery — a structural feature relevant to valuing NioCorp's scandium production, not just a data-quality quirk.

**Titanium (TiO₂):** Not found in available filings or in the external research conducted — TiO₂ is a large, well-established global market, but no company-specific or independently-sourced TAM figure was gathered for this report; NioCorp's own filings describe its planned TiO₂ output as "a small quantity... relative to other producers" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A], implying it will be a price-taker in this market regardless of overall market size.

NioCorp's own filings do not provide an independent, company-cited third-party market-size estimate for any of its three target commodities — not found in available filings.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

**Niobium — CBMM dominance is a named, filing-level risk.** [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "The world's largest supplier of niobium, Companhia Brasileira de Metalurgia e Mineração, supplies approximately 85% of the world's niobium. Any attempt to suppress the price of niobium by such supplier... would have negative consequences on the price of niobium and, potentially, on our value." (Note: from external source) CBMM is privately held — majority-owned by Brazil's Moreira Salles family (~70%), with a Japanese/Korean consortium (~15%) and a Chinese consortium led by CITIC Metal (~15%) holding the remainder (Note: from external source, Wikipedia / geopoliticalmining.com / BNamericas, 2026). This is structurally significant: the dominant global supplier is not a public company NioCorp can benchmark against on standard equity metrics, and it has a Chinese ownership stake despite being Brazilian — a nuance relevant to any "non-China supply chain" framing of NioCorp's investment case (see Section 4).

**Scandium — fragmented, byproduct-sourced supply.** [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A]: "Supply of scandium has been sporadic in recent years, and there are no primary scandium mines in the world at present. Production primarily occurs as a by-product from existing metallurgical plants, primarily in Russia, Canada, the Philippines, and China." Notably, Russia and China — both geopolitically sensitive supply sources for U.S.-based end users — are named as primary current sources, reinforcing the "supply gap" argument for a primary scandium producer, but also meaning NioCorp would be entering a market with essentially no established primary-producer benchmark to price against.

**Titanium** — no named competitors in the filings; not found in available filings.

**Peer companies (external, not SEC-filing-sourced):** (Note: from external source, as of various 2026 dates) Relevant North American critical-minerals development/production peers include MP Materials (MP, ~$9.81B market cap, producing rare earths at Mountain Pass, expanding into magnets with DoD offtake), USA Rare Earth (USAR, ~$1.32B, development stage), Ramaco Resources (METC, ~$1.02B, dual-platform operating met coal + developing Ga/Ge/Sc/REE critical minerals at its Brook Mine, WY project), Ucore Rare Metals (~US$240-275M, RapidSX separation technology, demo-stage), and Rare Earths Americas (REA, ~$463M, exploration stage, recent Nasdaq listing). None of these directly target niobium as a primary product — NioCorp's specific positioning as a would-be Western niobium producer is largely unique among its closest North American critical-minerals peers, most of which are rare-earth-magnet-chain focused. ## 3. Regulatory Environment

NioCorp operates at the intersection of state-level Nebraska mining/environmental permitting and, increasingly, U.S. federal critical-minerals policy and financing programs.

**Permitting regime (Nebraska):** Air Construction Permit (issued June 2020, no further extensions required per NDEE), Johnson County Special Use Permit (issued December 2019), and a Clean Water Act Section 404 permit via USACE's Nationwide Permit Program are held; the Company states "No other NEPA-level federal permits are now expected to be required" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2]. Three discretionary Nebraska state permits remain outstanding: Solid Waste Permit, Air Operating Permit, and — most notably — a Radioactive Materials License from the Nebraska Department of Health and Human Services (NDHHS), an engagement the company describes as ongoing "since 2014" [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 2] — over a decade without resolution, a genuine permitting-timeline risk rather than routine boilerplate.

**Federal financing/government-relationship threads:**
- **EXIM Bank:** Up to $800 million debt-financing application submitted June 2023, sitting at step two of EXIM's four-step approval process as of the FY2025 filing [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1A] — not yet committed.
- **DoD Agreement:** Milestone-contingent reimbursement of up to $10.0 million via the Defense Industrial Base Consortium (signed August 4, 2025, a subsequent event relative to the FY2025 fiscal year-end) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 1].
- No explicit "Defense Production Act" reference or formal government "critical minerals" list citation was found in any of the three 10-Ks reviewed — not found in available filings, despite this being a common framing in external/press coverage of the company (see [[Valuation Check]]).

**Broader critical-minerals policy backdrop (external, not from filings):** (Note: from external source) China refines roughly 90% of global rare earths and is the dominant refiner for 19 of 20 minerals analyzed in the IEA's Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2025, representing ~70% of global processing capacity overall (Note: from external source Fortune, March 11, 2026, citing IEA data). The EU's 2024 Critical Raw Materials Act aims to reduce China dependency via domestic production/refining and international partnerships, and in February 2026 the U.S. held critical-minerals talks with 54 countries plus the EU on coordinated investment, permitting, and joint financing (Note: from external source ODI.org / Policy Options, March 2026). This policy environment is the macro tailwind underpinning NioCorp's EXIM/DoD engagement, though it is worth noting that this same policy urgency is also driving well-capitalized competitors like MP Materials (which already has a confirmed 10-year DoD magnet offtake) — the tailwind is shared across the sector, not exclusive to NioCorp.

## 4. Industry Structural Notes

- NioCorp would be a genuinely novel Western entrant in the niobium market specifically — CBMM's ~85% global share (per NioCorp's own filing) means there is essentially no diversified competitive set to benchmark a "second niobium producer" against; NioCorp's own filings flag CBMM's pricing power as a direct risk to NioCorp's economics, not just an industry-structure footnote.
- The scandium market's complete absence of primary producers (a fact NioCorp's own filings state directly) cuts both ways: it is a genuine opportunity to be first-to-market as a primary supplier, but it also means there is no established primary-producer price/cost benchmark, and market size estimates for scandium specifically show unusually wide dispersion across external sources (5x+ range in some comparisons) — a sign of how immature and thinly-covered this specific commodity market is.
- Regulatory risk in this sector is not generic boilerplate: NioCorp's permitting path is a specific, multi-year, still-incomplete sequence with one permit (Radioactive Materials License) outstanding for over a decade, and its financing path runs directly through two specific, not-yet-finalized U.S. government relationships (EXIM, DoD) rather than conventional capital markets alone.
- The company's peer set (MP Materials, USA Rare Earth, Ramaco, Ucore) is broadly in the same "Western critical-minerals diversification" policy tailwind, but MP Materials in particular is already a confirmed producer with a locked-in DoD magnet offtake — a materially more advanced stage than NioCorp's pre-construction, pre-final-financing status. Investors should not conflate sector-level policy tailwinds with company-specific execution risk, which remains substantial for NioCorp specifically (see [[Bear Case]]).
