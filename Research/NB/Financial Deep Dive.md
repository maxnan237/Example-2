# NioCorp Developments Ltd. (NB) — Financial Deep Dive

*Analysis date: 2026-08-04*

NioCorp is a pre-revenue, development-stage company. There is no product revenue, no gross margin, and no traditional operating-leverage analysis to perform — this document focuses on cash burn, financing activity, capitalized project costs, balance-sheet liquidity, and dilution, which are the financial metrics that actually matter at this stage. All figures are in thousands of U.S. dollars unless noted; NioCorp reports in USD (confirmed by each financial statement header: "expressed in thousands of U.S. dollars"). Fiscal year ends June 30.

## 6. Going Concern

The going-concern qualification is **materially consistent across all three fiscal years** — it has not been resolved or softened, despite the FY2025 cash improvement.

**FY2025** (Deloitte & Touche LLP, Denver CO, PCAOB ID #34, dated September 11, 2025) [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firms]: "The accompanying financial statements have been prepared assuming that the Company will continue as a going concern. As discussed in Note 4 to the financial statements, the Company has an accumulated deficit and does not generate any revenue. Further, the Company does not have sufficient cash on hand to fund normal operations as well as the construction necessary for mine development for the next twelve months. These conditions raise substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern."

[NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Note 4 Going Concern Issues]: "...has concluded that management's plans do not alleviate substantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a going concern."

**FY2024** [NB_10K_FY2024.htm, Report of Independent Registered Public Accounting Firms]: "The accompanying consolidated financial statements have been prepared assuming that the Company will continue as a going concern... These conditions raise substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern." — essentially identical language to FY2025.

**FY2023** [NB_10K_FY2023.htm, Item 1A Risk Factors]: "The notes that accompany our consolidated financial statements for the year ended June 30, 2023, disclose that substantial doubt exists as to our ability to continue as a going concern... We currently have no historical recurring source of revenue and our ability to continue as a going concern is dependent on our ability to raise capital..."

**Assessment (analyst interpretation, inferred from the pattern across three years): the going-concern qualification has NOT improved from FY2023 to FY2025**, even though cash grew roughly 13x year-over-year (from $2.0M to $25.6M). This is because the relevant benchmark is not "near-term operating cash," but the ~$1,141M Elk Creek construction capex requirement — [NB_10K_FY2025.htm, Item 7]: "the doubt cannot be alleviated with confidence until the Company has entered into a business climate where funding of its planned ongoing operating activities is secured." A materially stronger cash position still leaves the company far short of what full-scale construction requires.

## 7. Liquidity and Capital Resources (MD&A)

- "As of June 30, 2025, the Company had cash of $25.6 million and working capital of $24.8 million, compared to cash of $2.0 million and a working capital deficit of $9.0 million on June 30, 2024."
- "The Company's current planned cash needs are approximately $40.0 million to $50.0 million for the next twelve months."
- Yorkville Equity Facility Financing Agreement provides up to ~$46.9 million of additional access but "expires by its terms on April 1, 2026."
- A Maxim Placement Agency Agreement (July 17, 2025) restricts variable-rate financings through September 17, 2025.

**FY2024:** Working-capital deficit of $9.0M disclosed; net cash used in operations $11.7M.

**FY2023** [NB_10K_FY2023.htm, Item 7]: "As of June 30, 2023, the Company had cash of $2.3 million and working capital of $0.2 million" — "The Company's current planned cash needs are approximately $11.8 million until June 30, 2024" (~$685,000/month average planned expenditure). "The Company anticipates that it does not have sufficient cash on hand to continue to fund basic operations for the next twelve months, and additional funds totaling $8.0 million to $9.0 million... are likely to be necessary."

**Trend:** the stated 12-month forward cash need grew substantially — from ~$11.8M (FY2023 plan) to $40-50M (FY2025 plan) — even as the company's cash position improved dramatically, reflecting a shift in spending intensity from pure exploration-stage costs toward pre-construction/EXIM-diligence-driven Elk Creek project costs (the drilling campaign, the feasibility-study update, and permitting work).

## Key Financial Takeaways

1. **Zero revenue, zero debt, improving but still constrained liquidity.** NioCorp ended FY2025 debt-free with $25.6M cash — the strongest balance sheet in its recent history — but that is still roughly 2% of the ~$1,141M initial capex the Elk Creek project requires per its own (now-dated) feasibility study.
2. **Going concern has not been resolved**, and by the company's own framing will not be resolved until construction financing (debt + equity) is actually secured — cash-on-hand improvements alone don't change the auditor's or management's own conclusion.
3. **Dilution has been aggressive and is explicitly expected to continue.** Shares outstanding rose 54% in FY2025 alone, and combined warrants+options equal ~56% of shares outstanding at FYE2025 — before accounting for the even larger dilution disclosed in external reporting for FY2026 (see [[Valuation Check]]).
4. **A material weakness in internal controls over financial reporting** was identified as of the FY2025 fiscal year-end, a governance flag that should weigh on confidence in the company's financial reporting as it scales toward a much larger capital structure.
