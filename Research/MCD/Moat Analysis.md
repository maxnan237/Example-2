# McDonald's Corporation (MCD) — Moat Analysis

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02*

Rate each moat source as **Strong / Moderate / Weak / Absent** with evidence.

---

## 1. Switching Costs — **Strong**

This is unusual for a consumer-facing business but very real for MCD's actual customers-of-record: franchisees. [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Business Summary]: franchise/license agreements "generally have 20-year terms," and [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Franchise Arrangements]: "At the end of the 20-year franchise arrangement, the Company maintains control of the underlying real estate and building." A franchisee who has invested capital in equipment, signage, and local goodwill under the McDonald's brand faces enormous switching costs to exit — they would forfeit both the real estate (owned/leased by MCD) and the brand equity built over two decades. [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Franchise Arrangements]: contracted future minimum rents receivable total $31,451M through the mid-2030s and beyond — a highly visible, multi-decade locked-in franchisee revenue stream.

For end consumers, switching costs are low (fast food is a low-commitment purchase), but the loyalty program (targeting 250 million 90-day active users by 2027, per [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Strategic Direction]) is a deliberate attempt to build habitual/repeat-purchase behavior.

**Rating: Strong.** The franchisee-side switching cost (real estate control + 20-year terms) is a genuine structural moat; consumer-side switching costs are low but not the primary lock-in mechanism of the business model.

---

## 2. Network Effects — **Absent**

A hamburger restaurant does not become more valuable to one customer because another customer is also eating there. MCD's business does not benefit from classic network effects. Loyalty-program data (aggregated purchase behavior informing personalization/marketing) is a mild data-scale advantage but does not constitute a network effect in the traditional sense.

**Rating: Absent.** Not a relevant moat source for a physical restaurant/franchise business.

---

## 3. Cost Advantages — **Strong**

[MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Supply Chain, Food Safety and Quality]: "The Company and its franchisees purchase food, packaging, equipment, and other goods from numerous independent suppliers... The Company has a comprehensive strategy that its global supply chain organization leverages to identify, assess, and manage risk." With 45,356 restaurants generating $139.4 billion in systemwide sales (per [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, 2025 Financial Performance]), MCD's purchasing scale gives it negotiating leverage with suppliers that few competitors can match — Yum! Brands (KFC/Taco Bell/Pizza Hut) is the closest scale peer, while Wendy's and most fast-casual chains operate at a fraction of MCD's unit count.

MCD's own margin structure evidences this: consolidated operating margin of 46.1% (FY2025) is exceptionally high for a restaurant business, driven by the largely fixed-fee (rent + royalty) franchised-revenue model layered atop scale-driven supply chain economics.

**Rating: Strong.** Global purchasing scale and the largely fixed-cost franchised-rent economic model together produce an operating margin most competitors cannot replicate.

---

## 4. Intangible Assets — **Strong**

[MCD_10K_FY2025.htm]: "the Company considers the 'McDonald's' trademark and the Golden Arches Logo to be of material importance to its business." The Golden Arches are among the most recognized brand symbols globally. [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Risk Factors]: "Failure to preserve the value or relevance of our brand could have an adverse impact on our financial results" — management itself frames brand value as central, and by extension, as a real (if intangible and unquantified) asset.

Real estate ownership is also, functionally, an intangible-adjacent moat: [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Restaurant Development and Capital Expenditures]: "the Company owned approximately 56% of the land and approximately 80% of the buildings for restaurants in its consolidated markets" — a globally distributed, largely irreplaceable real estate portfolio built over 70+ years of site selection.

**Rating: Strong.** Global brand recognition plus an owned real estate portfolio that cannot be quickly or cheaply replicated by a new entrant or challenger brand.

---

## 5. Efficient Scale — **Moderate**

The QSR category is large and globally fragmented enough (per [[Industry Scan]], estimated at $1.1-1.2 trillion in 2026 depending on methodology) that efficient-scale protection is only partial — Yum! Brands, Restaurant Brands International, and regional/national chains all operate profitably alongside MCD. However, at the hyper-local level (a specific intersection or neighborhood), the economics of opening a second major QSR drive-thru location directly adjacent to an established, high-volume McDonald's are often unattractive, giving MCD a degree of local efficient-scale protection restaurant-by-restaurant even though the category-wide market supports many large competitors.

**Rating: Moderate.** Global market is not efficiently-scale-protected against large competitors, but individual site economics provide some local protection against hyper-local competition.

---

## Moat Trajectory: **Stable**

### Evidence for trajectory
1. Operating margin has been remarkably stable and rising (45.7% FY2023 → 45.2% FY2024 → 46.1% FY2025, per [[Financial Deep Dive]]) — no evidence of margin erosion from competitive pressure.
2. Unit growth continues at a healthy pace: 45,356 restaurants (2025) vs. 41,822 (2023), +8.4% over two years, with a stated target of 50,000 by end of 2027 [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Strategic Direction].
3. 50 consecutive years of dividend increases through FY2025 — an unusually long track record that itself signals durable, non-cyclical earnings power underlying the moat.
4. Franchisee real-estate/contract lock-in (20-year terms, $31.5bn of contracted future rents) has not weakened in structure.

### Narrowing risks
1. **Declining after-tax ROIC**: 25.2% (2023) → 21.8% (2024) → 20.3% (2025) — a meaningful ~490bp decline over two years [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm], suggesting incremental capital (rising capex, the Grand Foods Holding equity stake increase) is generating lower returns than the legacy asset base, even as the moat's qualitative features remain intact.
2. **Consumer value/traffic pressure**: (Note: from external source) reported double-digit declines in low-income consumer traffic and management commentary that consumer sentiment "may be getting a little bit worse" — if sustained, this pressures the franchisee-sales base that ultimately funds MCD's rent/royalty revenue, even though it is a demand-cycle risk rather than a structural moat erosion.

---

## Moat Summary

MCD's moat is unusually durable for a consumer restaurant business because its true "customer" for switching-cost purposes is the franchisee, not the end diner — and franchisees face 20-year contractual lock-in plus forfeiture of real estate control if they exit. Combined with global purchasing scale, an owned real-estate portfolio, and one of the most recognized brands in the world, MCD scores Strong on four of five classic moat sources (network effects being structurally irrelevant to the business). The trajectory is Stable rather than Widening, however, because declining ROIC and reported consumer-traffic softness among lower-income households suggest the moat's economic output — not its structural integrity — is under some near-term pressure.
