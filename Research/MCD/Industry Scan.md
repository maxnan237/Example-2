# McDonald's Corporation (MCD) — Industry Scan

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02*

## 1. Market Size and Growth

(Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) Estimates for the global quick-service restaurant (QSR) market vary substantially by provider: Fortune Business Insights estimates ~$1.14-1.16 trillion in 2026, growing to $2.3-2.55 trillion by 2034/2035 at a CAGR of roughly 9.1-9.2%; a narrower Market Research Intellect estimate cites a $450+ billion market reaching ~$520 billion by 2033 at 4.7% CAGR. This wide dispersion reflects differing market-definition scope (some include fast-casual and delivery-only concepts, others don't) and should be treated as directional only.

(Note: from external source) A key structural trend cited across providers: approximately 60% of QSR sales globally now flow through mobile apps, kiosks, or online/delivery platforms — consistent with MCD's own filed emphasis on "Digital, Delivery, Drive Thru" as strategic pillars (see [[Business Overview]]).

MCD's own filings do not provide an independent third-party market-size estimate. Not found in available filings: a company-disclosed TAM figure.

## 2. Competitive Landscape

[MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Competition]: "McDonald's restaurants compete with international, national, regional and local retailers of traditional, fast casual and other food service competitors. The Company measures its competitive position within the informal eating out ('IEO') segment, which is inclusive of the Company's primary competition of quick-service restaurants, but also includes 100% home delivery/takeaway providers, convenience stores, street stalls or kiosks, cafés, specialist coffee shops, self-service cafeterias and juice/smoothie bars. The Company competes among quick-service restaurants primarily on the basis of price, convenience, service, experience, menu variety and product quality." → Notably, **no specific competitor is named anywhere in the three 10-Ks reviewed** — not Burger King, Wendy's, Starbucks, Chipotle, or any other brand. ## 3. Regulatory Environment

[MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Legal Proceedings]: "The Company has pending a number of claims and lawsuits that have been filed in various jurisdictions... While the Company does not believe that any such claims, lawsuits, laws or regulations will have a material adverse effect on its financial condition or results of operations, unfavorable rulings could occur." Disclosed categories: franchising disputes, supplier disputes, employee disputes (wage/hour, discrimination), customer disputes (product/nutrition/advertising claims), IP litigation, and government regulation (advertising, franchising, health, safety, environment, competition, zoning, employment, taxation) — no individually named or quantified pending matter in the filing itself.

[MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Government Regulations]: "While costs associated with legal and regulatory compliance have increased along with the number and scope of laws and regulations affecting our business, these costs are not expected to have a material effect on the Company's capital expenditures, earnings or competitive position."

**Specific active litigation (external sources, not disclosed by name/detail in the 10-K):**
- (Note: from external source, hoodline.com) E. coli outbreak litigation linked to the 2024 Quarter Pounder slivered-onion outbreak remains active in 2026.
- (Note: from external source) A racial discrimination lawsuit brought by former executives Vicki Guster-Hines and Domineca Neal was cleared to go to jury trial in September 2026 in Chicago federal court.
- (Note: from external source, HR Dive) A no-poach franchise hiring-agreement lawsuit (dating to 2017) was resolved/settled in 2026.

The gap between the filing's generic "pending claims" language and the specificity of these external reports is itself notable — investors relying solely on the 10-K would not learn the names or nature of these specific matters.

## 4. Industry Structural Notes

- MCD's own risk factors [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Risk Factors] flag "intense competition" generically but, unlike TME's filings (which named NetEase directly amid active litigation), MCD's 10-K does not identify any single competitor as a distinct threat — consistent with a mature, diversified competitive landscape rather than a duopoly-style rivalry.
- The QSR industry's digital/delivery shift (60%+ of sales via app/kiosk/online, per external sources) aligns with MCD's own capital allocation: FY2025 capex of $3,365M (up from $2,357M in FY2023, see [[Financial Deep Dive]]) is being directed substantially toward digital, delivery, and drive-thru infrastructure per the company's stated "4D" strategy.
- (Note: from external source) MCD's June 2026 "McDonald's > NEXT" growth strategy announcement (value pricing, loyalty perks, nostalgia-driven menu innovation) responds directly to reported low-income consumer traffic pressure — a demand-side industry dynamic (value-conscious consumers trading down or reducing visit frequency) that is broader than MCD alone and likely affects QSR peers similarly.
