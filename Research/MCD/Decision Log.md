# McDonald's Corporation (MCD) — Decision Log

*Initiated: 2026-07-02*

---

## Investment Thesis Summary

MCD is the world's largest QSR franchisor (45,356 restaurants, ~95% franchised), generating stable rent-and-royalty cash flows from a 20-year-contracted franchise system, trading near 52-week lows on reported consumer-traffic softness despite a 50-year unbroken dividend-growth streak.

**Bull thesis:** The franchise-model moat (real estate control, 20-year contract terms, brand, purchasing scale) is structurally intact and margin trends confirm it (operating margin 46.1% in FY2025, up from 45.7% in FY2023); the stock trades at a discount to most healthy QSR peers and well below sell-side consensus targets (~$329-354 average vs. ~$270 price); the "McDonald's > NEXT" value strategy directly targets the reported low-income traffic weakness; Q1 2026 results beat consensus on both revenue and EPS.

**Bear thesis:** After-tax ROIC has declined for two consecutive years (25.2%→20.3%) even as capex has risen 43% since FY2023, suggesting capital is being deployed at declining marginal returns; buyback execution slowed sharply in FY2025 despite a new $15bn authorization; the stock has underperformed the S&P 500 by roughly 40 percentage points over the trailing year; and the entire revenue base depends on franchisee sales health, which is directly exposed to the reported consumer pullback.

## Decision Framework

**To buy at ~$270:**
- Confirmation in Q2 2026 results (July 29, 2026) that the guided deceleration is temporary, not the start of a deeper slowdown
- Evidence the "McDonald's > NEXT" value strategy is stabilizing low-income consumer traffic
- Stabilization or improvement in the after-tax ROIC trend

**To avoid:**
- Continued ROIC decline alongside rising capex with no traffic stabilization
- A second consecutive year of sharply reduced buyback execution despite the $15bn authorization
- Evidence the low-income consumer pullback is structural (e.g., extending into middle-income cohorts)

**Current stance: Monitor**

MCD's moat is genuinely one of the strongest in this research vault — franchisee lock-in via 20-year contracts and real estate control is a structural advantage few QSR competitors can replicate, and the 50-year dividend streak reflects real underlying durability. But the valuation discount to peers and to sell-side targets is not obviously a mispricing: it coincides with two straight years of declining ROIC, a sharp buyback slowdown, and reported consumer-traffic softness that management itself has acknowledged. This is a "wait for the next data point" situation rather than an immediate buy or an outright avoid — the July 29, 2026 Q2 earnings release is a near-term, well-defined catalyst that will meaningfully clarify whether the demand softness is stabilizing or deepening.

**Key triggers for position changes:**
- Q2 2026 earnings (July 29, 2026) showing comparable sales stabilization → upgrade toward Value Play
- Continued ROIC decline + traffic weakness through Q3 2026 → downgrade toward Avoid
- Return to FY2023-level buyback pace, signaling management confidence in current valuation → upgrade signal
