# MCD Investment Memo

**McDonald's Corporation (NYSE: MCD) | 2026-07-02**

---

## The Business in 30 Seconds

McDonald's is the world's largest quick-service restaurant franchisor — 45,356 restaurants across 100+ countries, ~95% franchised, earning stable rent-and-royalty revenue from franchisees operating under 20-year contracts, with McDonald's retaining control of the underlying real estate as its core structural lever [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Business Summary / Franchise Arrangements].

---

## What's Going Right

**Franchise model produces exceptional, stable margins.** Consolidated operating margin rose from 45.7% (FY2023) to 46.1% (FY2025) [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, MD&A], driven by a largely fixed-fee rent/royalty revenue base that is far less volatile than a typical restaurant operator's cost structure.

**50 consecutive years of dividend increases.** [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, MD&A]: "The Company has paid dividends on its common stock for 50 consecutive years and has increased the dividend amount every year," with the FY2025 quarterly dividend rising 5% to $1.86/share ($7.44 annualized).

**Real estate and contract lock-in provide genuine switching-cost protection.** MCD owns ~56% of land and ~80% of buildings in consolidated markets, with $31,451M of contracted future minimum rents receivable from existing franchise agreements [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Restaurant Development and Capital Expenditures / Franchise Arrangements].

**Q1 2026 results beat consensus.** (Note: from external source) Revenue of $6.52B (+9% YoY) and EPS of $2.83 both topped estimates, with global comparable sales of +3.8% broad-based across all three segments.

**Valuation discount to healthy peers and sell-side targets.** Trailing P/E of ~22-23x sits below Yum! Brands, Starbucks, and Chipotle (Note: from external source); average analyst price target of $329-354 implies ~22%+ upside from the current ~$270 price.

---

## What Could Go Wrong

**After-tax ROIC has declined for two consecutive years** — 25.2% (FY2023) → 21.8% (FY2024) → 20.3% (FY2025) [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm] — even as capital expenditures rose 43% over the same period, indicating incremental capital is generating lower returns than the legacy asset base.

**Reported consumer-traffic softness, particularly among lower-income households.** (Note: from external source, PYMNTS) CEO Chris Kempczinski has stated consumer sentiment "certainly not improving, and it may be getting a little bit worse," with low-income traffic reportedly down double digits — not quantified in MCD's own 10-K.

**Buyback execution slowed sharply in FY2025** — 6.7 million shares repurchased for $2.0 billion, down from 11.1 million shares/$3.1 billion in FY2023 — despite a new $15.0 billion authorization approved in November 2024 [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm].

**Negative shareholders' equity.** Total shareholders' equity (deficit) was $(1,791)M at FY2025 year-end [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, Consolidated Balance Sheet] — narrowing from prior years but still negative, meaning enterprise value rests entirely on future cash-flow confidence rather than a tangible net-asset floor.

**Management has guided to a "meaningful deceleration" in Q2 2026 comparable sales** (Note: from external source), a specific near-term catalyst (reporting July 29, 2026) that could confirm or dispel the demand-softness narrative.

**Significant 1-year stock underperformance.** (Note: from external source) MCD's 1-year total return is approximately -10% versus the S&P 500's approximately +30% — a roughly 40-percentage-point gap.

## Decision

**Current stance: Monitor**

MCD offers one of the strongest structural moats in this research vault — a franchise system built on 20-year contracts and real estate control that few QSR competitors can replicate, backed by a 50-year unbroken dividend-growth record. The stock's discount to peers and to sell-side price targets is real, but it is not obviously a pure sentiment mispricing: two consecutive years of declining ROIC, a sharp FY2025 buyback slowdown despite a fresh $15bn authorization, and reported (if not company-quantified) softness among lower-income consumers all point toward the same conclusion — the market is pricing in real near-term demand and capital-efficiency concerns, not just overreacting to noise. The July 29, 2026 Q2 earnings release, following management's own guidance for a "meaningful deceleration," is a well-defined near-term catalyst that should materially clarify whether this is a temporary comparison effect or the start of a deeper slowdown. Until then, this is a wait-for-confirmation situation on an otherwise high-quality, moat-protected business.

**Key triggers to watch:**
- Q2 2026 earnings (July 29, 2026) — does comparable-sales growth stabilize or deteriorate further?
- Any reversal in the two-year after-tax ROIC decline
- Return to a faster buyback pace, signaling management confidence in the current price
- Confirmation (or dispelling) of the low-income consumer traffic narrative in company disclosure, not just external reporting

**Key risks to monitor:**
- Structural (not cyclical) shift away from traditional QSR spending among value-conscious consumers
- Continued capital misallocation if rising capex fails to restore ROIC
- Franchisee financial stress cascading into the contracted $31.5bn future rent stream
- Active litigation (E. coli outbreak claims, discrimination lawsuit set for September 2026 trial, per external sources) creating headline or reputational risk beyond the amounts currently reflected in the filings
