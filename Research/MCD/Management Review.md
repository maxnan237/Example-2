## 2. Capital Allocation

**Buybacks.** [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm]: "On November 21, 2024, the Company's Board of Directors approved a share repurchase program, effective January 1, 2025 with no specified expiration date, that authorized the purchase of up to $15.0 billion of the Company's outstanding common stock. In 2025, approximately 6.7 million shares were repurchased for $2.0 billion under the program." Buyback pace has slowed markedly: 11.1M shares/$3.1bn (FY2023) → 10.1M shares/$2.8bn (FY2024) → 6.7M shares/$2.0bn (FY2025), even as a large new $15bn authorization began. This deceleration, occurring alongside a stock price near 52-week lows (Note: from external source), is worth monitoring — it could reflect capital discipline given rising capex needs, or a signal that management is not viewing the current depressed price as an obvious buying opportunity.

**Dividends.** [MCD_10K_FY2025.htm, MD&A]: "The Company has paid dividends on its common stock for 50 consecutive years and has increased the dividend amount every year." The FY2025 quarterly dividend rose 5% to $1.86/share in Q4 2025, equating to a $7.44 annualized rate — a genuine Dividend King track record ([[Financial Deep Dive]]) that has continued uninterrupted through the current demand softness.

**Capital expenditure growth.** Capex has risen from $2,357M (FY2023) to $3,365M (FY2025), a 43% increase over two years, concentrated in the International Operated Markets segment ($1,340M→$2,048M) — consistent with the stated unit-growth and digital/drive-thru investment strategy, but this rising capex intensity coincides with the declining ROIC trend flagged in [[Financial Deep Dive]] and [[Moat Analysis]].

**Assessment:** Capital allocation remains shareholder-friendly in form (uninterrupted 50-year dividend growth streak, active buyback authorization) but the FY2025 slowdown in actual buyback execution, combined with rising capex and falling ROIC, suggests management may be prioritizing unit growth and international expansion over near-term capital returns — a reasonable long-term bet, but one that has not yet shown up as improved returns on the incremental capital deployed.

## 5. Overall Assessment

Management has sustained one of the longest dividend-growth track records in the market (50 consecutive years) through a period of genuine demand headwinds, and Q1 2026 results show the business still beating near-term expectations despite reported consumer softness. However, three threads warrant continued monitoring: (1) the sharp FY2025 slowdown in buyback execution despite a large new authorization and a depressed stock price, (2) rising capex alongside two consecutive years of declining after-tax ROIC (25.2% → 21.8% → 20.3%), suggesting the incremental returns on new capital are not yet matching the legacy asset base, and (3) a compensation trajectory (CEO pay up to ~$20.6M in 2025, per external reporting) that has continued rising even as the stock has underperformed the S&P 500 by a wide margin over the trailing year (see [[Valuation Check]]) — not disqualifying, but worth weighing against operating performance when assessing management's alignment with shareholders in the current environment.
