## 1. Market Size and Growth

(Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) The global dry bulk shipping market is estimated at ~$12.74B (2026), projected to reach ~$16.22B by 2030 (6.2% CAGR). The Capesize segment specifically represents ~40% of the dry bulk market and is projected to grow at ~4.3% CAGR (2026-2035) per one industry report.

**Supply/demand balance appears roughly neutral.** (Note: from external source, BIMCO) Dry bulk ship demand growth is forecast at 2-3% in 2026, with supply growth of ~2.5% — a broadly balanced market, neither a strong tailwind nor a glut scenario.

**Underlying cargo trends are mixed.** (Note: from external source) Iron ore trade — Capesize's primary cargo — is forecast to grow only 0.5%-1.5% (2025-2027), a weak/soft outlook. Coal trade is forecast to **decline** 3.5%-4.5% over the forecast period as China shifts toward renewables, despite continued coal-plant capacity additions for energy security reasons. Partially offsetting this: sailing distances are expected to lengthen 0.5%-1.5%/year due to rising South Atlantic-to-Asia iron ore/bauxite flows, which is structurally supportive of Capesize tonne-mile demand even with soft absolute cargo volume growth.

**Freight rate indices (as of July 2, 2026, external source):** Baltic Dry Index (BDI) at 2,562 points — up 2.4% on the day to a near one-week high, with a ~25% gain for Q2 2026 but a ~22% decline in June 2026 alone, illustrating high short-term volatility. Baltic Capesize Index (BCI) at 3,692 points, up 4.1% on the day (second consecutive up day).

SHIP's own filings do not provide independent third-party market-size estimates — not found in available filings.

## 4. Industry Structural Notes

- The dry bulk shipping industry is a classic cyclical, capital-intensive, commodity-like business — freight rates (BDI/BCI) swing sharply on short notice (e.g., BCI +25% for Q2 2026 followed by BDI -22% in June alone, per external sources), and SHIP's near-total reliance on index-linked charters means its revenue directly tracks this volatility with limited smoothing, unlike a peer with more fixed-rate charter coverage.
- SHIP has disclosed ~50% of its fleet fixed at $29,000/day for 2026 (Note: from external source, referencing company guidance) — a partial hedge against the downside of the index-linked exposure, though the majority of the fleet remains rate-cycle-exposed.
- The industry's supply-side dynamics (BIMCO's ~2.5% 2026 supply growth forecast) suggest no acute overcapacity crisis is imminent, but the demand side is bifurcated: iron ore (weak growth) vs. coal (declining) — a genuinely mixed cargo outlook that argues against assuming a strong structural tailwind for Capesize rates going forward.
- SHIP's newbuilding program (5 vessels, all scrubber-fitted, deliveries 2027-2029) positions it to gradually lower average fleet age and improve fuel-efficiency compliance, but the current fleet (14.7 years average age and rising) is a genuine competitive disadvantage relative to peers investing more aggressively in fleet renewal today.
