# Seanergy Maritime Holdings Corp. (SHIP) — Management Review

*Analysis date: 2026-07-02*

---

## 1. Leadership

**CEO: Stamatios (Stamatis) N. Tsantanis.** (Note: from external source — not from uploaded filings) Chairman and CEO, with more than 23 years of experience in shipping and capital markets. Also serves as CEO of **United Maritime Corporation**, a related shipping company — a dual-role structure directly relevant to the related-party transactions discussed below. Precise CEO start date/tenure was not confirmed via external search — flagged as a gap.

**Compensation:** [SHIP_20F_FY2025.htm, per FY2024 20-F, referenced via external secondary source]: individual executive compensation is **not separately disclosed**, consistent with common practice for Marshall Islands foreign private issuers. Aggregate compensation paid to all executive officers and directors as a group was $2.0 million for the year ended December 31, 2024; aggregate director fees alone were $0.5 million in both FY2024 and FY2023 (up from $0.4 million in FY2022). No individual breakout for Tsantanis is available — a genuine transparency limitation relative to U.S. domestic issuers.

---

## 2. Governance — Super-Voting Preferred Stock (Material Concern)

[SHIP_20F_FY2025.htm]: 20,000 Series B preferred shares were issued to CEO Tsantanis in December 2021 for a total consideration of **$250**, carrying **25,000 votes per share**, capped at 49.99% of aggregate voting power [[Financial Deep Dive]]. This structure gives the CEO near-majority, contractually-entrenched voting control for a nominal cash outlay, disproportionate to his economic (common share) ownership. Combined with his role as CEO of the related party United Maritime Corporation, this is a governance structure that concentrates control in a single individual across two related companies simultaneously.

(Note: from external source) A 2024 proxy contest — an entity reportedly linked to the Economou family opposed Seanergy's board nominees (Dimitrios Anagnostopoulos and Ioannis Kartsonas) at the 2024 Annual Meeting; Seanergy's nominees were re-elected per a November 2024 press release. External search could not confirm whether a similar contest recurred in 2025 or 2026 — flagged as an open item. The mere occurrence of a shareholder proxy fight, regardless of outcome, is itself a signal that governance concerns have been raised by at least one sophisticated market participant.

---

## 3. Related-Party Transactions — United Maritime Corporation

[SHIP_20F_FY2025.htm, Item 3.D]: "We depend on officers and directors who are associated with United Maritime Corporation... which may create conflicts of interest." Multiple FY2025-FY2026 transactions involve United Maritime directly: the M/V Dukeship 18-month bareboat charter-out (Feb 2026), the M/V Squireship agreed sale ($29.5M, pending March 2026), and a $2.0 million bridge loan to United (per the business-extraction agent's findings). [[Business Overview]] notes that special committees of disinterested directors reportedly negotiated these deals — a standard governance safeguard — but the underlying structural conflict (the same CEO leading both companies) persists regardless of the review process used for any individual transaction.

**Assessment:** this is a material governance flag, not a minor technicality. Investors should weight the related-party dependency and super-voting structure heavily when assessing minority-shareholder protection at Seanergy.

## 4. Capital Allocation

**Fleet renewal strategy.** Management has committed to a 5-vessel newbuilding program (~$460 million combined program value per external sources), financed through cash on hand and sale-and-leaseback arrangements — a reasonable strategic response to the aging-fleet competitive disadvantage identified in [[Moat Analysis]], though it also adds meaningful execution risk (delivery timing, cost overruns) and further leverages the balance sheet.

**Variable, cycle-linked dividend.** [[Financial Deep Dive]]: dividend per share fell from $0.76 (FY2024) to $0.33 (FY2025), a 57% decline tracking the TCE rate/margin decline directly — management has been willing to cut the dividend when earnings power declines, rather than maintaining an unsustainable payout, which is a credible (if unglamorous) capital discipline signal for a cyclical business.

**Continued share issuance.** An active ATM program (up to $30 million aggregate) and ongoing stock-based compensation have driven steady, moderate share dilution (~16% cumulative growth in shares outstanding, Jan 2023-Dec 2025) — a persistent, if modest, drag on per-share value that shareholders should weigh against the fleet-growth funding rationale.

## 5. Insider Ownership and Recent Transactions

(Note: from external source) CEO Tsantanis owns 2,084,403 shares (~9.62% of shares outstanding as of March 2026), making him the company's second-largest shareholder by common equity — separate from and in addition to his super-voting preferred stake. Total insider ownership is estimated at ~27-33%, with retail/individual investors holding the largest single bloc (~51%). Institutional ownership includes Renaissance Technologies, Susquehanna International Group, Walleye Capital, Millennium Management, and Goldman Sachs Group among ~55 reporting institutional holders.

(Note: from external source) General commentary suggests net insider buying in the trailing period, but specific transaction dates/amounts were not resolved via external search — not found in available filings or confirmed external sources with sufficient precision.

## 6. Guidance Credibility

SHIP does not appear to issue formal forward financial guidance in the manner of a US domestic issuer (consistent with typical shipping-company disclosure practice) — not found in available filings. (Note: from external source) The company has disclosed that approximately 50% of its fleet is fixed at $29,000/day for 2026, providing a partial, verifiable forward revenue floor that can be checked against actual results as the year progresses — this is a more useful, concrete forward data point than qualitative guidance would be for a commodity-exposed business.

---

## 7. Overall Assessment

Management has executed a credible operational turnaround — three consecutive years of positive net income, no vessel impairments despite a declining-rate year, and a disciplined, cycle-linked dividend policy that cuts payouts when earnings soften rather than over-committing. However, the governance structure is a genuine, material concern: a super-voting preferred stake acquired for nominal consideration gives the CEO outsized, entrenched control, and that same individual simultaneously leads a related company (United Maritime) with which Seanergy conducts recurring transactions. Investors considering SHIP should treat the governance and related-party risk as a first-order consideration, not a secondary footnote — it directly affects how much confidence to place in management's stewardship of minority shareholders' capital, independent of the company's demonstrated operational competence.
